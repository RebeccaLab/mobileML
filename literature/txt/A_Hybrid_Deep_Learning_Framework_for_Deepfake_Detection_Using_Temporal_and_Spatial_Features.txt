ABSTRACT The rise of deep-fake technology has sparked concerns as it blurs the distinction between fake
media by harnessing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). This has raised issues surrounding privacy
and security in the realm. This has led to a decrease in trust during online interactions; thus, emphasizing
the importance of creating reliable methods for detection purposes. Our research introduces a model for
detecting deepfakes by utilizing an Enhanced EfficientNet B0 structure in conjunction with Temporal
Convolutional Neural Networks (TempCNNs). This approach aims to tackle the challenges presented
by the evolving sophistication of deep-fake techniques. The system dissects video inputs into frames to
extract features comprehensively by using Multi Test Convolutional Networks (MTCNN). This method
ensures face detection and alignment by focusing on facial regions. To enhance the model’s adaptability,
to different scenarios and datasets we implement data augmentation techniques such as CutMix, MixUp and
Random Erasing. These strategies help the model maintain its strength, against distortions found in deepfake
content. The backbone of EfficientNet B0 utilizes Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolutions (MBConv)
and Squeeze and Excitation (SE ) blocks to enhance feature extraction by adjusting channels to highlight
details effectively. A Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) facilitates the fusion of scale features capturing
intricate details as well, as broader context. When tested on the FFIW 10 K dataset, which comprises 10,000
videos evenly split between manipulated content, the model attained a training accuracy of 91.5 % and a
testing accuracy of 92.45 %, after 40 epochs. The findings showcase the model’s proficiency, in identifying
videos with precision and tackling the issue of class imbalances found in datasets – a valuable contribution,
to advancing dependable deepfake detection solutions. Furthermore, the model achieves an impressive
balance between accuracy and computational efficiency, attaining 92.45% testing accuracy with a lightweight
computational cost of 0.45 GFLOPs, making it a highly practical choice for real-world deployment.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
The field of deepfake detection and related computer vision
applications has seen significant advancements in recent
years, with various models and techniques being developed
to improve accuracy and efficiency. The literature in this
domain highlights the evolution of approaches, datasets,
and performance metrics, demonstrating how research has
progressively tackled the challenges posed by deep-fake
technology.
Deepfake detection has leveraged Convolutional Neural
Networks (CNNs) and hybrid models to achieve high detec-
tion accuracy. A 2024 study utilized Inception Net in a
CNN-based comparative analysis, trained on a Kaggle dataset
comprising 401 videos and 3,745 images post-augmentation,
achieving an accuracy of 92.3% with moderate computational
efficiency [6]. Similarly, a 2023 study applied Support Vector
Machines (SVM) with Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
on a custom dataset, transforming RGB images to YCbCr,
leading to a 92.8% accuracy when using PCA, compared
to 72.2% without PCA [8]. A 2025 study further enhanced
face forgery detection by introducing attention-based fea-
ture learning and Optimized Long Short-Term Memory
(OLSTM). Using datasets such as FaceForensics++, Deep-
fake Detection Challenge, Celebrity Deepfake, and Wild
Deepfake, this approach achieved a cross-validated accu-
racy of 94.65%, albeit at a lower efficiency due to its
reliance on OLSTM and ResNet-50 Multi-Head Attention
(RN50MHA) [9].
Recent comparative analyses have shed light on the
performance of various deepfake detection architectures.
In 2024, researchers compared CNN+RNN, Deepfake
Detection Network (DAFDN), Hybrid Inception ResNet v2,
and Xception models on a comprehensive dataset. The
study reported accuracy rates of 94.8% for CNN+RNN,
97.8% for DAFDN, and 98% for the Hybrid model, with
efficiency varying based on computational demands [11].
Similarly, a 2022 study assessed multiple deepfake detec-
tion algorithms on FaceForensics++, reporting an accuracy
of 96%, indicating that efficiency trade-offs are a key factor
in model selection [20]. Further, Generative Adversarial
Networks (GANs) have played a crucial role in image syn-
thesis and deep-fake generation. A 2020 review of GAN
variants in computer vision examined datasets such as
LSUN-BEDROOM,
CELEBA-HQ-128,
and
CIFAR-10,
reporting a precision of over 90% for BigGAN, though
computational costs were noted as a challenge [7]. Like-
wise, a 2018 study implemented Enhanced Super-Resolution
Generative Adversarial Networks (ESRGAN) using Residual
Dense Blocks (RDDB) without Batch Normalization (BN)
layers. Despite achieving an RMSE score of 15.15% on the
PIRM self-validation dataset, the model’s high complexity
made it less efficient [10].
On the other hand, Alternative deepfake detection meth-
ods have incorporated diverse techniques. A 2022 study
employed MesoNet on FaceForensics++ and Celeb-DF,
yielding an 88% accuracy while maintaining high efficiency
due to its lightweight nature [13]. Another 2022 study focused
on Point of Interest (POI) action detection using Multi-task
Cascaded Convolutional Networks (MTCNN) and MesoNet
on a customized dataset of Barack Obama, achieving an

across the video.
B. GLOBAL AVERAGE POOLING, CLASSIFICATION, AND
TEMPORAL ANALYSIS
Following the extraction of pertinent spatial features to the
segments by the backbone, a Global Average Pooling (GAP)
Layer processes the resultant data. Individual map of features
is reduced to just one scalar value by the GAP layer, which
also decreases the spatial dimensions of the mapped features.
This procedure guarantees every segment’s spatial data is
reduced in a summary vector. After that, an entirely connected
layer via regularization for dropouts receives the output
from the GAP layer. By independently eliminating neurons
throughout training, dropout guarantees that the algorithm
applies well to new data and prevents overfitting. A temporal
convolutional network (TCN) receives the highly dense fea-
ture vector produced by the fully connected layer to perform
temporal evaluation. To represent long-range time-dependent

IV. MATERIALS AND METHODS
A. DATASET
The dataset we’ve used for our experiment is FFIW 10K [23]
which consists of 10,000 videos, with an equal distribution
of 5,000 fake and 5,000 real videos. This ensures that there is
no class imbalance and this also sets our dataset apart from
many other deepfake datasets, such as Deepfake TIMIT [24],
Celeb-DF [25], FaceForensics++ [2], and DFDC [26].
For example, the Deepfake TIMIT dataset contains about
320 real and 640 fake, Celeb-DF contains 590 real and
5639 fake videos, FaceForensics++ contains 1000 real and
4000 fake videos, and DFDC contains 104500 fake videos
and 23654 real videos. These class imbalances can introduce
bias during model training. In comparison to these datasets,
FFIW 10K provides a more balanced dataset which ensures
the model makes it more reliable and generalized, and a
good benchmark for training deepfake detection models.
This dataset also contains high-quality videos which
enhances its utility for research in computer vision and deep
learning.
The dataset used in this study, FFIW 10K, consists of
10,000 videos, with an equal distribution of 5,000 real and
5,000 fake videos, ensuring no class imbalance. This bal-
anced dataset sets it apart from other datasets like Deepfake
TIMIT, Celeb-DF, FaceForensics++, and DFDC, which
typically suffer from unequal distributions, often skewed
toward fake videos. For instance, Deepfake TIMIT has more
fake videos, which could introduce bias during training.
The balanced nature of FFIW 10K helps the model train
on a more reliable dataset, improving its generalization and
reducing bias. To further enhance adaptability and robustness,
several data augmentation techniques were applied, including
CutMix, MixUp, and Random Erasing. CutMix combines
two different images by cutting and pasting them together,
which helps the model learn robust features by exposing
it to diverse data combinations. MixUp generates synthetic
samples by averaging two images, encouraging the model to
learn smoother decision boundaries and reducing overfitting.
Random Erasing randomly selects and erases parts of an
image, making the model focus on other important regions,
which helps it recognize features even when parts of the
input are missing or altered. These augmentation techniques,
combined with the balanced nature of FFIW 10K, ensure that
the model is more adaptable to varying deepfake manipula-
tions and can generalize better to real-world scenarios where
videos may have subtle changes or distortions.

achieving an efficiency of 0.45 GFLOPs (Giga Floating Point
Operations per Second). This efficiency is the result of several
key architectural optimizations:
1) EFFICIENTNET B0 BACKBONE
Our
model
utilizes
the
EfficientNet
B0
architecture,
which employs Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolutions
(MBConv) to significantly reduce the number of operations
without compromising feature extraction quality. The depth
wise separable convolutions within MBConv blocks fur-
ther minimize computational load by reducing redundant
operations [27].
2) DYNAMIC INPUT SCALING
The model adapts input resolution based on the segment’s
complexity—higher-resolution processing for intricate seg-
ments and lower-resolution processing for simpler ones. This
ensures computational resources are allocated efficiently,
preventing unnecessary overhead.
3) FEATURE PYRAMID NETWORK (FPN) FOR MULTI-SCALE
FUSION
By incorporating an FPN, our framework merges spatial
information across different layers, reducing the need for

The TCN efficiently models temporal inconsistencies by

high-end GPUs for inference, our approach ensures that
deepfake detection remains accessible, fast, and scalable.
