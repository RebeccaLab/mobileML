A B S T R A C T
The rapid advancement of deepfake technology poses significant threats to the integrity and privacy of 
biometric systems, such as facial recognition and voice authentication. To address this issue, there is an urgent 
need for advanced forensic detection methods that can reliably safeguard biometric data from manipulation and 
unauthorized access. However, current methods mainly focus on shallow feature extraction and neglect feature 
refinement and enhancement, which leads to low detection accuracy and poor generalization performance. 
To address this problem, we propose Feature Refinement and Enhancement Network (FRENet) for deepfake 
detection by leveraging progressive refinement and enhanced mixed feature learning. Specifically, a Low 
Rank Projected Self-Attention (LPSA) module is introduced for the refinement and enhancement of features. 
Also, a Patch-based Focused (PatchFocus) module is proposed to highlight local texture inconsistencies in 
key regions. In addition, we propose a Refine Fusion (RefFus) module that integrates the refined features 
and associated noise information to enhance feature separability. Experimental results across five benchmark 
datasets demonstrate that the proposed FRENet outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both accuracy 
and generalization. The code is available at https://github.com/weichengsong-code/FRENet.
1. Introduction
Biometric authentication, such as facial, fingerprint, and voice 
recognition, has become widely used in everyday smart devices. These 
technologies provide users with efficient and secure access to digital 
services. However, the growing reliance on biometric systems has 
also increased their exposure to sophisticated security threats [1,2]. 
In particular, recent advances in generative adversarial networks and 
diffusion models [3,4] have greatly improved the realism and diver-
sity of facial forgeries. As a result, distinguishing between authentic 
and manipulated biometric data has become increasingly challeng-
ing. This raises serious concerns about the security and reliability of 
biometric authentication systems in the face of emerging deepfake 
technologies [5].
Recent studies have shown that deepfake attacks can successfully 
bypass state-of-the-art facial recognition systems, which pose serious 
threats such as unauthorized access, identity theft, and privacy inva-
sion [6,7]. In addition, multiple reports have documented incidents of 
financial fraud and misinformation campaigns driven by deepfake tech-
nology, which have caused significant economic and societal harm [8].

In this work, we propose a deepfake detection model termed Fea-

generalization ability by refining and enhancing the feature. FRENet 
integrates three key modules to support progressive feature enhance-
ment. The Low Rank Projected Self-Attention (LPSA) module refines 
and enhances global features through a self-attention mechanism. The 
Patch-based Focused (PatchFocus) module applies localized attention 
to capture fine-grained texture features and subtle inconsistencies. This 
allows the proposed model to focus on key regions that are most 
susceptible to forgery. To further boost discriminative capability, the 
Refine Fusion (RefFus) module aggregates the refined features and asso-
ciated noise information. This fusion enhances feature separability and 
facilitates more accurate discrimination between real and fake content. 
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
• We propose the FRENet model to enhance generalization by 
refining features and improving the model for detecting subtle 
forgery traces.
• We propose an LPSA module to enhance feature representation 
by emphasizing distinctions between real and forged content. 
This module guides the model to focus on subtle yet informative 
discrepancies. Additionally, a PatchFocus module is built to con-

can improve the accuracy of the forgery artifact detection.
• We introduce a RefFus module that fuses the refined features and 
associated noise information to enhance feature separability and 
improve discriminative performance.

2. Related work
In recent years, deepfake detection has received increasing atten-
tion due to the rapid advancement of generative techniques. Various 
methods have been proposed, which can be categorized as spatial-
frequency domain fusion-based methods, feature Enhancement and 
refinement-based methods, and local attention-based methods.
2.1. Deepfake detection based on spatial-frequency domain fusion
Early detection methods primarily focused on inconsistencies within 
the spatial domain and abrupt transitions in the frequency domain. 
For example, Rössler et al. [21] introduced a forgery detection net-
work based on the Xception architecture. It uses depthwise separable 
convolutions to capture local and global patterns, which helps detect 
subtle deepfake artifacts. Li et al. [22] incorporated frequency-domain 
analysis and identified fusion boundaries in images to detect subtle 
artifacts.
To combine the advantages of both spatial and frequency domains, 
Zhai et al. [23] proposed a dual-stream frequency-spatial fusion net-
work that combines spatial and frequency-domain information to im-
prove deepfake detection. However, the method may incur significant 
computational costs due to the simultaneous processing of two distinct 
streams. Meanwhile, Zheng et al. [24] introduced a spatial-frequency 
cross-fusion model. However, the model may experience performance 
degradation when processing noisy or low-quality data. Yi et al. [25] 
proposed a deepfake detection method, termed Spatial-Frequency Fea-
ture Integration (SFIAD), to fuse spatial and frequency domain features 
and uses dynamic boundary optimization. While it improves detection 
accuracy on multiple datasets, it still faces challenges in handling 
highly compressed or low-quality images. Liu et al. [18] introduced 
the Spatial-Phase Shallow Learning (SPSL) method. The method demon-
strates computational efficiency, requiring less processing power than 
more complex models. However, its performance remains suboptimal in 
complex environments. This issue is mainly attributed to limited feature 
extraction capabilities and a lack of comprehensive understanding of 
fine-grained details. Consequently, the design of a feature enhancement 
module is essential for refining and strengthening the generalization of 
the detector.
2.2. Deepfake detection based on feature enhancement and refinement
With the continuous advancement of deepfake technology, tradi-
tional detection methods face growing challenges in accurately iden-
tifying high-quality forged images. These forgeries often exhibit fewer 
visual artifacts and maintain strong semantic consistency. To address 
these limitations, recent research has increasingly focused on feature 
enhancement and refinement techniques. These methods aim to im-
prove the ability of the model to capture subtle and complex differ-
ences between real and fake content. Strategies such as multi-scale 
feature extraction and frequency-domain representations enhance the 
quality and discriminability of learned features. These improvements 
lead to greater robustness and accuracy in deepfake detection across 
diverse scenarios. For example, Zhao et al. [26] proposed a multi-
attentional deepfake detection network with feature enhancement to 
improve discriminative representation. Their method integrates multi-
scale attention with enhanced feature fusion to effectively boost de-
tection performance in complex forgery scenarios. Zhou et al. [27] 
proposed a frequency-aware method to improve forgery detection by 
fusing spatial and frequency components. Lai et al. [28] introduced 
a multi-feature fusion method for deepfake video detection, which 
extracts features from the spatial, frequency, local gravity pattern, 
and time domains. Qiu et al. [29] proposed the d2fusion model that 
enhances deepfake detection by fusing spatial and frequency domain 
features with attention mechanisms. The method uses feature superpo-
sition and phase updating to improve the differentiation between real 
and forged content. Additionally, Lin et al. [30] developed a detection 
method that uses convolutional neural networks to improve detection 
accuracy by using multiscale feature extraction. This method captures 
subtle inconsistencies across various levels of image detail, thereby 
improving the sensitivity of the model to complex forgeries.
2.3. Deepfake detection based on local attention
Attention mechanisms play a crucial role in deepfake detection. 
They can guide models to focus on the most informative regions of an 
image, thereby improving the identification of manipulated content. By 
assigning greater importance to salient features, attention mechanisms 
enhance the capacity of the model to capture subtle inconsistencies 
introduced during the forgery process. This targeted representation 
improves the accuracy and robustness of detecting both local and global 
artifacts.
Several studies have explored different attention-based strategies 
to enhance deepfake detection performance. Luong et al. [31] pro-
posed cross-modal attention to focus on the most relevant information

3. Proposed method
3.1. Overall framework
The overall architecture of the FRENet method is illustrated in Fig. 
1(a). The process begins with the input image being processed by 
PhaseNet, which reconstructs the image based on phase information 
extracted via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). This phase information 
helps capture the underlying structure and frequency characteristics 
of the image. After reconstructing the image, the Inverse Fast Fourier 
Transform (IFFT) is applied to fuse the reconstructed image with the 
original input. This fusion enhances both structural and frequency-
based features and improves the robustness and quality of the final 
image representation. The fused output is passed to the Xception net-
work [21]. Xception employs depthwise separable convolutions and 
residual connections to efficiently extract high-level features. Subse-
quently, the features undergo refinement via the LPSA module. The 
primary role of LPSA is to reduce the correlation between real and fake 
features, which enables the model to better distinguish genuine content 
from manipulations.
Additionally, a PatchFocus module is incorporated to guide FRENet 
in focusing on local details within the image, especially those areas 
likely to contain manipulation artifacts. This ensures that the model 
focuses on regions where forgery traces are most prominent, thereby 
enhancing its detection capabilities. The local attention mechanism 
is further refined through SoftPooling operations and convolutional 
layers, which ensure that the focus on critical regions is accurate and 
efficient. The RefFus module is then used to fuse the refined features 
with injected noise information. This noise injection enhances the 
separability of features, which enables the model to more effectively 
distinguish between real and fake content. It functions as a regular-
ization technique that improves the generalization of the model across 
diverse forgery types and varying conditions. Finally, a Multi-Layer 
Perceptron (MLP) classifier is employed to make the final distinction 
between real and fake images.

4. Experimental results and analysis
4.1. Implementation details
In this work, we use the Adam [36] optimizer and configure the 
optimizer with 𝛽1 = 0.9, 𝛽2 = 0.999, 𝜖= 1 × 10−8, and a weight decay of 
0.0005. The learning rate is set to 2×10−4 and the batch size is 32. We 
resize all input images to 256 × 256 pixels and normalize the images 
using a mean and standard deviation of [0.5, 0.5, 0.5]. The backbone is 
the official Xception model. We initialize the model weights with pa-
rameters pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset and adopt cross-entropy 
loss as the objective function. Several data augmentation strategies are 
adopted, for example, random horizontal flip with a probability of 0.5, 
random rotation in the angle range of [−10◦, 10◦], and random Gaussian 
blur with a probability of 0.5. The JPEG compression quality is uni-
formly sampled between 40 and 100. All experiments are carried out 
using the PyTorch library [37]. We conduct the training and evaluation 
on two NVIDIA 3090 Ti GPUs. The training runs for 10 epochs. We set 
the random seed to 1024 to ensure reproducibility.
4.2. Datasets
The experiments were trained in the FaceForensics ++ (FF ++) [21] 
dataset and evaluated on five benchmark datasets, which include 
FF++[21], Celeb-DF-v1 [38], Celeb-DF-v2 [38], DFDCP [39], and 
FaceShifter [40].
The FF++ dataset is a comprehensive resource for training and eval-
uating deep learning models in facial manipulation detection. It con-
tains 1000 original videos which includes 4000 forged videos generated 
using four representative facial forgery techniques, namely DeepFakes 
(DF), Face2Face (F2F), FaceSwap (FS), and NeuralTextures (NT). In 
total, the dataset contains over 1.8 million manipulated images, which 
are extracted video frames.
The Celeb-DF-v1 dataset contains 795 real videos featuring 59 
celebrity subjects, along with 5639 high-quality deepfake videos. This 
dataset incorporates early deepfake generation methods, such as neural 
network-based facial synthesis and blending.
The Celeb-DF-v2 provides high-resolution videos with an equal dis-
tribution of real and fake content, generated using advanced deepfake 
techniques. This makes the dataset a valuable benchmark for testing 
and improving deepfake detection methods.
The DFDCP dataset comprises high-quality real and manipulated 
videos generated using diverse facial forgery techniques. It emphasizes 
realism by including variations in lighting conditions, camera angles, 
and video resolutions. These characteristics render the dataset suitable 
for training and benchmarking robust deepfake detection models under 
practical conditions.
The FaceShifter dataset provides highly realistic face-swapping ex-
amples with minimal visual artifacts. It ensures consistency in lighting 
conditions, background alignment, and facial identity preservation.
4.3. Evaluation metrics
The proposed model is evaluated using four standard binary classi-
fication metrics, namely Area Under the Curve (AUC), Accuracy (ACC), 
Average Precision (AP), and Equal Error Rate (EER). These metrics 
provide a comprehensive assessment of the detection performance of 
the model from different perspectives. The AUC measures the ability of 
the model to distinguish between real and fake samples. It reflects the 
probability that the model correctly ranks a randomly chosen positive 
sample higher than a negative one. A higher AUC value indicates better 
separability between classes. The ACC quantifies the proportion of 
correctly classified samples over the total number of samples. It pro-
vides an overall measure of the effectiveness of the model in detecting 
deepfakes. The AP evaluates the trade-off between precision and recall 
across various decision thresholds. It is particularly useful for assessing 
the performance of the model in imbalanced classification scenarios. 
The EER represents the point at which the false acceptance rate equals 
the false rejection rate. A lower EER indicates better balance and 
reliability in classification, especially in biometric and security-related 
tasks.
4.4. Experiments and discussion
4.4.1. Cross-testing
To evaluate the generalization and robustness of the proposed 
FRENet approach, we conducted cross-dataset testing. The model was 
trained on the FF++ dataset and tested on four additional datasets, 
namely Celeb-DF-v1, Celeb-DF-v2, DFDCP, and FaceShifter. These
datasets provide a diverse range of deepfake content, which ensures 
that the evaluation covers a variety of forgery types, generation meth-
ods, and image qualities. The detailed results and comparisons are 
presented in Table 1. It proves that the FRENet outperforms other 
competitors in the four datasets. Specifically, compared to the baseline 
model, SPSL, the FRENet improves the AUC by 8.56%, 5.1%, 3.62%, 
10.5% in the Celeb-DF-v1, Celeb-DF-v2, DFDCP, and FaceShifter, re-
spectively. Meanwhile, it achieves an average increase of 5. 13% 
compared to SPSL.  Additionally, some competing methods show signif-
icant decreases in certain datasets. In contrast, FRENet maintains more 
stable AUC scores on all benchmarks. This stability indicates a strong 
robustness against domain shift and various forgery techniques.
To further validate the superiority of the proposed method, the 
FRENet model was trained on the FF++ dataset and evaluated on four

4.4.4. Ablation study
To evaluate the effectiveness of each key component of the proposed 
model, we conducted ablation experiments on the Celeb-DF-v1 dataset. 
In these experiments, the baseline model for comparison is SPSL [18]. 
The main components analyzed include LPSA, PatchFocus, and RefFus. 
LPSA serves as the feature refinement module, which reduces the 
correlation between real and fake features and thus enhances discrim-
inability. PatchFocus acts as a local attention module that guides the 
model to focus on regions prone to forgery. RefFus is the feature 
enhancement module, designed to fuse refined features with auxiliary 
information to improve feature separability and robustness. The results 
of the ablation experiment are presented in Table 4.
It shows that the introduction of the single LPSA module improves 
the performance of AUC, AP and EER, but the ACC decreases slightly. 
This outcome indicates that the LPSA module improves the ability 
to distinguish between real and fake images, while the classification 
accuracy shows minor fluctuation. When the RefFus module is inde-
pendently equipped, the four indicators are all improved to certain 
extent. It demonstrates that RefFus enhances both detection and clas-
sification performance. When LPSA and RefFus are both equipped, the 
improvements are more evident. The indicators of AUC, ACC, and AP 
are enhanced by 3.67%, 2.05%, and 1.61%, respectively. Meanwhile, 
the EER is decreased by 3.82% compared to baseline. The combination 
of PatchFocus and RefFus yields moderate improvements. The AUC, 
ACC, and AP are increased 2.25%, 1.51%, and 0.64%, respectively, and 
EER is decreased by 1.83%. These improvements are less significant 
than those obtained with the combination of LPSA and RefFus. Overall, 
the integration of LPSA, RefFus, and PatchFocus achieves the best 
performance. The AUC, ACC, and AP are increased by 6.56%, 5.33%, 
and 3.04%, respectively, and EER is decreased by 6.81% relative to 
baseline. These ablation results demonstrate that each proposed module 
contributes significantly to the overall performance of the model. The 
modular design leads to consistent and substantial improvements in 
deepfake detection, enhancing both robustness and accuracy under 
varied forgery conditions.
4.4.5. Hyperparameter study
This section investigates the influence of the learning rate and the 
weight decay on the performance of the model. Table 5 presents the re-
sults of varying these hyperparameters. The learning rate values include 
0.0001, 0.0002, and 0.0003. The weight decay values include 0.0004, 
0.0005, and 0.0006. The experimental results show that hyperparame-
ter selection directly affects the model AUC. FRENet achieves the best 
performance with a learning rate of 0.0002 and a weight decay of 
0.0005, where the AUC reaches 88.48%. In comparison, both lower and 
higher values of these hyperparameters result in reduced performance. 
Based on the experimental findings, subsequent experiments adopt the 
best-performing learning rate and weight decay values.

lightweight versions for edge deployment. The extension of the method

methods may further improve adaptability. These strategies can help 
the model handle new forgery techniques without large amounts of
