Enhancing multimodal deepfake detection with local–global feature
integration and diffusion models
Muhammad Javed1 · Zhaohui Zhang1 · Fida Hussain Dahri2 · Teerath Kumar3
Received: 31 December 2024 / Revised: 11 February 2025 / Accepted: 15 February 2025 / Published online: 11 March 2025
© The Author(s) 2025
Abstract
Deepfake detection has become a critical challenge nowadays with the rise of sophisticated generative techniques that manip-
ulate audio-visual data. Existing methods primarily focus on lip movement synchronization using audio and visual features,
often relying on local feature extraction with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). In this work, we propose an enhanced
multimodal framework that integrates with local and global features for advanced deepfake detection. Our approach extends
traditional pipelines by introducing additional visual features such as eye movement and facial regions, combined with audio
features to model cross-modal dependencies. While CNNs capture local features, Vision Transformers (ViTs) extract global
contextual relationships from both visual and audio modalities. The diffusion models are incorporated as pre-processors to
reﬁne noisy data and generate realistic augmentations, ensuring high-quality feature representation. The proposed framework
achieves state-of-the-art performance, with accuracy scores of 0.9987, 0.9825, 0.9915, and 0.9812 on the FakeAVCeleb,
AV-Deepfake1M, TVIL, and LAV-DF datasets, respectively. These results demonstrate signiﬁcant improvements over exist-
ing methods, highlighting the framework’s superior generalization and robustness in detecting subtle inconsistencies across
manipulated audio-visual data.
Keywords Audio-visual synchronization · Deepfake detection · Diffusion models · Multimodal analysis · Vision transformers

2 Related work
The growing challenge of detecting deepfake content has led
to extensive research on various detection methods, focusing
on both audio-visual synchronization and multimodal fusion
approaches. Early methods in deepfake detection relied pri-
marily on analyzing individual modalities, such as video or
audio, to identify inconsistencies or artifacts [15]. However,
as deepfake technology has advanced, more sophisticated
techniques have emerged that combine multiple modalities,
such as both visual and auditory features, to achieve higher
accuracy in detection [15].
2.1 Multimodal deepfake detection
Multimodal deepfake detection approaches have gained
attentionduetotheirpotentialtoenhanceaccuracybyanalyz-
ing both visual and audio features simultaneously. A notable
work in this area introduced an audio-visual synchronization
framework to detect inconsistencies between lip movements
and audio features. This approach uses Convolutional Neu-
ral Networks (CNNs) and Temporal Convolutional Networks
(TCNs) for processing visual and audio data, respectively,
and employs Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiL-
STM) [16] networks combined with Dynamic Time Warping
(DTW) to assess synchronization [17]. However, these meth-
odsprimarilyfocusonlatefusionstrategies,whichmayfailto
capture complex temporal dependencies between the modali-
ties, limiting their effectiveness in certain deepfake scenarios
[17].
Further research has extended this approach by incorpo-
rating various fusion techniques, including hybrid models
and ensemble methods. For instance, some studies have inte-
grated CNNs and RNN-based architectures to capture both
spatial and temporal features [18–20]. While these methods
offer improvements in detection performance, they still face
challenges in handling real-time applications and ensuring
robustness across different types of deepfake manipulations
[21].
2.2 Deepfake detection via feature fusion
Several other studies have explored feature fusion strate-
gies to combine the strengths of different feature extraction
methods. For example, some researchers have proposed com-
bining CNN features for visual processing with recurrent
models like BiLSTM to capture temporal dynamics in the
audio [14, 22, 23]. In this context, deep learning models
that focus on ﬁne-grained analysis of lip synchronization
have shown promising results [21]. However, issues related
to computational efﬁciency and real-time processing remain
signiﬁcant hurdles in the adoption of these methods in prac-
tical applications [24].
2.3 Innovations in audio-visual synchronization
One signiﬁcant contribution to deepfake detection involves
leveraging the natural synchronization between audio and
visual signals. The work of [17] explored the use of
multimodal learning techniques to model inconsistencies
between the video and audio. This method primarily detects
manipulation by identifying misalignments in audio-visual
synchronization, providing an important step forward in dis-
tinguishing real content from fabricated media [18, 19].
However, integrating these features with high accuracy and
in real-time systems remains a complex task [20, 21, 25].

2.4 Advancements in deepfake generation
techniques
Recent studies have also highlighted various techniques for
generating deepfakes, such as face synthesis and expres-
sion swapping, which have furthered the complexity of
detection tasks [21–23]. The ability to swap faces, alter
expressions, and manipulate audio tracks independently of
video has led to signiﬁcant challenges in deepfake detection
[14]. Some efforts have attempted to address this by using
hybrid models, combining traditional face recognition tech-
niques with advanced deep learning models [26, 27]. Despite
these advancements, detecting deepfakes involving subtle
alterations, like mismatched lip-sync or ﬁne-grained audio
manipulations, still poses a major challenge [28, 29].
2.5 Recent detection frameworks
Several modern deepfake detection frameworks focus on
multimodal fusion, leveraging both video and audio fea-
tures to detect inconsistencies in real-time applications. For
instance, the study presented by [18, 30] integrates both video
frames and audio features through deep neural networks, pro-
viding promising results on datasets like FaceForensics++
and CelebDF. However, the need for large datasets and the
computational demands of these methods can hinder their
practical deployment [31, 32].
In contrast, our proposed approach focuses on a ﬁne-
grained analysis of lip synchronization by combining visual
features, such as eye movements and facial regions, with
audio features. By using Vision Transformers (ViTs) for
global feature extraction and Diffusion Models to reﬁne both
audio and visual inputs, our framework aims to improve
detection accuracy while ensuring computational efﬁciency.
We evaluate our model on multimodal datasets such as
FakeAVCeleb [11], AV-Deepfake1M [12], TVIL [13], and
LAV-DF [14], which provide diverse and challenging data
for detecting complex deepfake manipulations.
These work highlights the rapid progress in deepfake
detection techniques, particularly through multimodal anal-
ysis. While signiﬁcant advancements have been made, chal-
lenges remain in ensuring real-time, robust, and scalable
detection systems. Our approach aims to build on these
advancements by focusing on precise lip-sync analysis, inte-
grating both visual and audio features, and reﬁning data
through Diffusion Models for improved accuracy and efﬁ-
ciency in deepfake detection.

visual and audio feature extraction, multimodal fusion, clas-

3.5 Preprocessing with diffusion models
To enhance the quality of both visual and audio inputs, we
employ Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)
as a preprocessing step. DDPMs iteratively reﬁne noisy data
into high-quality features, making them particularly effective
for handling low-quality or artifact-laden inputs.
For visual data, the DDPM reﬁnes noisy frames by revers-
ing a stochastic noise addition process:
q(xt−1|xt) = N(√αtxt, (1 −αt)I)
(14)
where xt is the noisy frame at time step t, αt is a predeﬁned
noise schedule, and N denotes a Gaussian distribution.
The denoised frame x0 is recovered through a learned
reverse process:
pθ(xt−1|xt) = N(μθ(xt, t), θ(xt, t))
(15)
where μθ and θ are the predicted mean and variance param-
eters of the denoising step, learned via a neural network.
For audio, the DDPM operates similarly, enhancing the
clarity of noisy spectrograms. Given an audio input a(t),
the diffusion model progressively reconstructs the original
signal:
a0 = D(at; θ)
(16)
where at is the noisy spectrogram, D is the learned denois-
ing process, and θ are the model parameters. This ensures
that audio features, such as Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefﬁ-
cients (MFCCs) [34], are extracted from high-quality inputs,
improving the overall robustness of the framework.
The use of DDPMs enables the system to preprocess both
visual and audio data, ensuring that the downstream feature
extraction and classiﬁcation stages operate on high-ﬁdelity
inputs.

The experiments were implemented in PyTorch and executed
on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. The training used the Adam

Early stopping and a learning rate scheduler ensured stable
convergence. We used metrics such as accuracy, precision,
recall, F1-score, and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) to
evaluate the model’s performance.

4.5 Limitations
While the proposed framework demonstrates signiﬁcant
improvements in deepfake detection, certain aspects warrant
further exploration. The integration of Vision Transformers
(ViTs) and diffusion models, although effective in capturing
global dependencies and reﬁning noisy inputs, introduces
increased computational complexity, potentially impacting
real-time applicability. Additionally, the reliance on high-
quality training data may limit the generalization capabil-
ity when applied to low-resolution or highly compressed
videos, which are prevalent in real-world scenarios. Further-
more, despite the framework’s robustness against existing
manipulation techniques, its adaptability to unseen deepfake
generation methods remains an open challenge, necessitat-
ing further evaluation on diverse datasets. The incorporation
of diffusion models as pre-processing steps, while beneﬁ-
cial for feature enhancement, also adds an additional layer
of computational overhead, which may not be suitable for
deployment in resource-constrained environments. Address-
ing these challenges in future work could further enhance the
efﬁciency and generalizability of the proposed approach.

5 Conclusion
Deepfake detection is a growing challenge due to advanced
generative techniques. We propose an enhanced multimodal
framework combining local and global features for robust
detection, using visual cues (e.g., eye movement, facial
regions) and audio. Vision Transformers (ViTs) capture
global context, while diffusion models improve prepro-
cessing and feature representation. Our framework outper-
forms existing methods, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy
on the FakeAVCeleb, AV-Deepfake1M, TVIL, and LAV-
DF datasets. Ablation studies highlight the importance
of ViTs and diffusion models, with pretrained ImageNet
weightsboostingperformance.Comparedtoframeworkslike
AVSFF, our approach excels in precision, recall, and F1-
score.
This framework offers a scalable solution for deepfake
detection, with future work focusing on adapting it to new
techniques and real-time performance.
