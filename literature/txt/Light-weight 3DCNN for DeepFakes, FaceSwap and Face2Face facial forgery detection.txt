Light-weight 3DCNN for DeepFakes, FaceSwap
and Face2Face facial forgery detection
Aditi Kohli1 · Abhinav Gupta2
Received: 27 July 2021 / Revised: 11 November 2021 / Accepted: 21 February 2022
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2022
Abstract
Detecting facial forgery in real time application such as video conferencing, netbanking,
verification of identity etc. is a major concern. Highly accurate facial forgery detectors
will be incompatible with limited computation and storage space devices like smartphones,
tablets, PCs due to their deep complex architectures. Therefore, we propose a light weight
3D convolutional neural networks (3DCNN) to detect popular facial forgeries namely,
DeepFakes, Face2Face and FaceSwap. The proposed 3DCNN is a five layered architec-
ture with only 2.69M trainable parameters. The proposed network learns spatio-temporal
features to detect forged videos. Further, to understand the detection process of 3DCNN,
activation maps are studied in detail. The popular FaceForensic++ dataset is employed to
train and test the proposed method. In addition, the effectiveness of the proposed method is
evaluated and compared with state-of-the art methods in terms of both, number of trainable
parameters and binary detection accuracy.
Keywords 3DCNN · Face forensics
1 Introduction
With the evolving deep learning techniques and current work from home scenarios, video
conferencing for business purpose, facial verification, virtual social connects have become
common. These deep learning techniques have made life easier, however, increasing mali-
cious use of it has led to an increase in facial forgeries. Thus, designing efficient and real
time compatible facial forgery detectors is of prime importance.
The most popular facial forgeries are namely, DeepFakes, Face2Face and FaceSwap.
DeepFakes and FaceSwap are identity based forgery while Face2Face is an expression
based forgery. The facial manipulations are even employed using feature rejuvenation, aging
and anthropometric simulations [4] and [5]. In literature, the researchers have employed

almost 22M trainable parameters. Similarly, network based on pre-trained VGG-16 [21],

compatible with limited computation and space devices, such as smartphones, personal lap-
tops and tablets. Whereas, lightweight deep learning models are easier to train and require
less overheads to update when deployed on smartphones, personal laptops and tablets.
Consider a video conferencing application, which is popularly and widely used on PCs,
laptops and smartphones. Many important business and personal discussions are done
through video conferencing. Let’s consider two types of video conferencing applications,
the first application consists of a small and lightweight facial manipulation detector while, the
second does not have any facial forgery detector. Therefore, if during the video conference,
someone with a forged face attends the conference, then only in the first type of application
fraud will be checked/detected. Thus, real world applications, highly demand lightweight
models. Therefore, this paper proposes a light weight 3D convolutional neural networks
(3DCNN) for facial manipulation detection. The proposed method does not employ complex
and computational expensive networks like FlowNets [9] or PWCNet [22] to extract tempo-
ral features of the test video. Rather exploit 3DCNN in a novel manner to extract both spatial
and temporal features. Furthermore, the proposed 3DCNN is designed with less number of
trainable parameters, which again results in a simple and light weight architecture.

1.
To design a light weight facial forgery detector using a novel, 3D convolutional neural
network (3DCNN).
2.
The 3D convolutional neural network (3DCNN) is employed in such a manner that it
initially learns spatial features and later learns the combined spatio-temporal features
of a face.
3.
Finally, the proposed method is compared with MobileNetV2 [19], XceptionNet [6] 3D
ResNet [26] and fCNN [16].

2 Motivation
Nowadays, the research emphasis is on designing small and efficient networks so that
computer vision applications can be brought to limited computation platforms, such as
smartphones, tablets, personal laptops, etc. In MobileNetV1 [14] and MobileNetV2 [19]
author reduced computation in initial layers by using depth wise separable convolution.
The lightweight MobileNet is applied for object detection, face recognition and other com-
puter vision applications. Further, it is compared with exiting models like VGG-16 [21],
GoogleNet [23] etc. for COCO object detection dataset. It is observed that MobileNet
achieves equivalent accuracy with reduced computation cost. Similarly, when compared
with popular FaceNet [20], the number of trainable parameters are reduced by half while
multiple-adds are reduced by eight times. However, the accuracy also drops by almost
4%. The same has been observed for deep fired covnets [27] and SqueezeNet [15], where
the authors designed the network to achieve equivalent or 2%-3% lower accuracy at
significantly lower computational cost.
Therefore, it can be inferred from the above that compromising with a slight decrease in
accuracy can significantly reduce the number of trained parameters. The resulting reduced
parameter model can be applied to real world applications. This prompted us to design a
computationally efficient and light weight 3DCNN network.
The proposed 3DCNN is extracting both spatial and temporal features without using
complex and computationally expensive optical flow methods. Rather employed simple
image processing tool, i.e x and y direction gradients. Rather simple image processing tools,
i.e. x and y direction gradients are employed. These x and y direction gradients present
strong candidates in the facial manipulation detection process. The Fig. 1 describes in detail
how the gradients in DeepFakes, FaceSwap and Face2Face faces are changing as compared
to the pristine face. The faces in Fig. 1 are extracted from pristine video number “216.mp4”
and their corresponding DeepFakes [8], Face2Face [24] and FaceSwap [10] videos of Face-
Forensic++ dataset [18]. The Fig. 1a-d represents faces from DeepFakes, ‘DF’, Face2Face,
‘F2F’ , FaceSwap, ‘FS’ and Pristine, ‘Pris’ videos. The two gradients Gx and Gy are
computed for the above four faces. The gradient Gx and Gy represent vertical edges and
horizontal edges in a face, respectively. The Fig. 1e-h represents Gx face gradients while
the Fig. 1i-l represents Gy face gradients of DF, F2F, FS and Pris, respectively.
On comparing gradients of DeepFakes with gradients of pristine, i.e comparing Fig. 1e
with h and i with l, it is visualized that there are more vertical and horizontal edges around
the cheeks and eyebrows. Similarly, in case of Face2Face, observe Fig. 1f and j with respect
to (h) and (l), abrupt edges can be seen around lips and forehead regions as this is a face
reenactment forgery. Likewise, in the case of FaceSwap, Fig. 1g and k, region around the
chin and mouth are exhibiting artifacts. Thus, high gradient values are observed in forged
facial regions as compared to pristine. This unique feature inspired us to employ gradients
in 3DCNN.
3 Proposed method
The proposed method is divided into two stages as shown in Fig. 2. The first is pre-
processing stage, where the video is converted into 4 depth matrix. This 4D matrix
comprises of two consecutive frames and its x and y direction gradients. The computed 4D
matrix is given as an input to 3DCNN in second stage. The second stage is employed to
detect the forged face using the proposed 3DCNN.

The conventional CNN is a 2 dimensional convolutional neural network, where features
are extracted from xy plane or spatial domain of input. However, to include features from
other domains as well, a 3 dimensional convolutional neural network is required. In 2D
CNN, the 2D kernels are convolved with input, thus output is a 2D. While in 3DCNN
the 3D kernels are convolved with an input cube formed by stacking three input layers.
Therefore, taking into account the combined effect of the xy plane and depth of input and
then generating a 2D output.

6 Conclusion
In the event of detecting forged facial videos, authors have begun to design complex archi-
tectures that are incompatible with real time applications. Therefore, a light weight network
is proposed to work with real time applications, such as laptops, smartphones etc. The
proposed method employs simple image processing tools such as gradients to pre-process
the face and further fed them to a novel five layered 3D covolutional neural network
(3DCNN) to classify between forged and pristine faces. The activation maps of proposed
3DCNN are visualized and studied in detail. The activation maps make inferences about
the discriminative features of face and forgery artifacts learned during training of proposed
3DCNN.
The proposed method is compared with Amerini et al. [1], Wang et al. [26], XceptionNet
[6], MobileNetV2 [19] and fCNN [16] methods in terms of binary detection accuracy and
trainable parameters. Comparative analysis shows that the proposed method achieves better
accuracy with only 2.69M of trainable parameters, which is about 10 times less than Wang
et al. [26] and XceptionNet [6].
