Enhancing Deepfake Detection Through Hybrid 
MobileNet-LSTM Model with Real-Time Image 
and Video Analysis 
 
 
 
Abstract—The race to crush information integrity and 
public trust is being won by one thing: deepfakes, AI 
manipulated media. The goal is to enhance the Deepfake 
detection using MobileNet V3 and LSTM network. MobileNet’s 
lightweight CNN architecture is able to induce the visual 
features in an image by an image and in a video and can pick up 
the slight visual clues of textures and facial structure. Some 
temporal inconsistencies that cannot be seen by image base 
methods are then analyzed using an LSTM network. The hybrid 
model is trained on real and deepfake media datasets, and is 
thus adaptable to emerging deepfake techniques. This has a user 
face interface to analyze the real time and fly media to get the 
analysis and analysis score and visual feedback of the identified 
artifacts. Unique to this system is its versatility for images and 
videos, and its real time capability, making it a suitable choice 
for practical use in social media, journalism, and law 
enforcement combating the spread of misinformation with a 
guarantee of digital media authenticity 
Keywords—media integrity, real-time detection, temporal 
modeling, image analysis, video analysis, mobilenet-lstm, 
deepfake detection. 
 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Advancements in artificial intelligence have produced 
deepfake tech which leverages to create highly convincing 
manipulated media that might be images or videos. 
Deepfakes started out as seen by some as a form of creative 
expression, but have quickly turned into a big concern in all 
fields—from politics to news media to social platforms. 
Deepfake algorithms have enabled voters to create synthetic 
content where they appear to perform actions or make 
statements they never did, and this medium has now become 
a powerful purveyor of misinformation, a reputation wrecker 
and a destroyer of public trust in digital information. 
 
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) at the heart of 
most deepfake generation techniques permit it to generate a 
person’s likeness or actions with extremely high precision. 
Although deepfake generation has become more accessible 
(and creates near authentic content with or without 
specialized technical expertise), this technology continues to 
grow in impact, questioning media authenticity and digital 
integrity. 
 
Most of the existing detection methods are trained to find 
visual inconsistencies or artifacts like the presence of facial 
articulation irregularity or some mismatch of lighting from 
one frame to another. Although very effective against 
standard manipulations, they do not work well against 
sophisticated deepfake algorithms aimed at eliminating or 
camouflaging such cues. In addition, image-based analysis is 
the main limitation of many systems, restricting them to 
analysis of sequential patterns (e.g., video-based contents). 
 
This work proposes a hybrid model for deepfake detection 
using MobileNet V3, a lightweight CNN optimized for 
spatial feature extraction and Long Short-Term Memory 
networks (LSTM) specialized for temporal analysis. 
Satisfyingly, MobileNet V3 can successfully localize spatial 
inconsistencies in individual frames, including texture 
anomalies 
and 
misaligned 
facial 
features. 
This 
is 
complemented by LSTM which analyzes sequential 
dependency among frames to detect temporal inconsistencies 
characteristic of manipulated videos. 
 
The proposed system addresses the problem using a 
unified approach using a combination of spatial and temporal 
features by integrating MobileNet V3 and LSTM. In 
MobileNet V3, each video frame is processed independently 
to extract spatial features, followed by sequential analysis by 
LSTM on the temporal pattern represented by the extracted 
spatial features. The system’s ability to capture subtle, 
sophisticated, and high value manipulations is improved due 
to this dual focus architecture.

fake images and videos created through many methods, the 
model is trained. This comprehensive training boosts 
adaptability to the future in the deepfake generation space. In 
addition, the lightweight design of the model supports near

II. LITERATURE SURVEY 
Deep learning techniques, particularly hybrid models, 
have been widely explored for video analysis, real-time 
recognition, and anomaly detection tasks. Abdullah et al. 
introduced a CNN-LSTM-based model for facial expression 
recognition in videos [1], which leverages the spatial features 
from CNNs and the temporal dependencies modeled by 
LSTMs, achieving notable success in video classification 
tasks. Similarly, Sánchez-Caballero et al. [2] proposed a 
recurrent neural network model that processes raw depth 
video data to perform real-time human action recognition, 
minimizing 
preprocessing 
overhead 
and 
improving 
efficiency. Expanding on these advancements, Mihanpour et 
al. [3] integrated DB-LSTM with ResNet for human action 
recognition in videos, demonstrating a robust approach that 
effectively combines feature extraction with temporal 
modeling. 
Ullah et al. employed CNN features alongside bi-
directional LSTMs to detect anomalies in real-time 
surveillance networks [4]. Their work highlights the ability 
of hybrid architectures to process complex surveillance data 
while ensuring real-time operation. Ghadekar et al. [5] 
enhanced anomaly detection in crowd scenes by leveraging 
ensemble 
learning 
techniques, 
further 
improving 
classification 
accuracy 
and 
robustness 
in 
dynamic 
environments. Addressing video forgery detection, Hashmi et 
al. explored a Conv-LSTM hybrid architecture for analyzing 
visual counterfeits [6]. Their results underline the 
significance of such models in tackling deepfake-related 
challenges. 
Yan et al. developed a self-attention eidetic 3D-LSTM for 
traffic flow forecasting [7], focusing on capturing long-term 
spatiotemporal dependencies. This work highlights the 
scalability of hybrid models in large-scale prediction tasks. 
Emotion recognition in videos has also been a significant area 
of research. Jagadeesh and Baranidharan [8] implemented a 
deep learning-based framework to analyze facial expressions 
of online learners in real-time, addressing critical challenges 
in the education domain. Khairdoost et al. [9] demonstrated 
the application of LSTMs for real-time driver maneuver 
prediction, providing actionable insights for intelligent 
transportation systems. 
Fire detection in videos was effectively addressed by 
Nguyen et al. [10], who proposed a multistage real-time 
detection framework using CNN-LSTM networks. This 
model ensured scalability and accuracy in emergency 
scenarios. Similarly, Rezaei and Yazdi [11] utilized deep 
learning methods to recognize crowd behavior in surveillance 
videos, showcasing the importance of video analysis in 
security applications. Natarajan et al. [12] extended video 
analysis capabilities by developing an end-to-end deep 
learning 
framework 
for 
sign 
language 
recognition, 
translation, and video generation, significantly contributing 
to accessibility technologies. 
Healthcare applications have also benefited from hybrid 
deep learning models. Yin et al. introduced MC-LSTM for 
real-time 3D human action detection, designed for intelligent 
healthcare systems [13]. This model provides rapid response 
capabilities and high accuracy, making it suitable for critical 
healthcare tasks. Video quality forecasting has been 
addressed by Dinaki et al. [14], who used multivariate time-
series deep learning models to predict video QoE, optimizing 
streaming services through better resource allocation. Kang 
and Kim [15] utilized 1D convolutional autoencoders to 
classify emotions in real-time using PPG and GSR signals, 
offering applications in wearable technology and emotion-
aware systems. 
In the domain of video emotion recognition, Zhang et al. 
[16] introduced a reinforcement learning-based approach that 
integrates domain knowledge, ensuring adaptability to 
varying video contexts. Anomaly detection in industrial IoT 
was addressed by Nizam et al. who implemented a deep 
anomaly detection framework using multivariate time-series 
data [17], enabling real-time decision-making in critical 
systems. Moreover, video-based action recognition was 
advanced by Mihanpour et al. [3], who combined ResNet and 
DB-LSTM to enhance performance in human action 
recognition tasks. 
Khairdoost et al. [9] contributed significantly to driver 
behavior prediction through LSTMs, addressing real-time 
challenges in autonomous vehicles. Similarly, Ghadekar et al. 
[5] utilized ensemble learning to analyze crowd scenes, 
improving the detection of anomalies in complex 
environments. Lastly, Yurtsever and Eken developed 
BabyPose, a system for decoding a baby’s non-verbal 
communication using 2D video-based pose estimation [18], 
which has applications in child healthcare and monitoring. 
Collectively, these studies emphasize the versatility and 
effectiveness of hybrid deep learning models like CNN-
LSTM and DB-LSTM across diverse applications, from 
video forgery detection to real-time healthcare and 
transportation systems. The reviewed literature provides a 
strong foundation for further research into enhancing 
deepfake detection through similar hybrid approaches, 
ensuring accurate and efficient real-time analysis.

III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY 
In this work, a complete deepfake detection model based 
MobileNet V3 and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) 
networks is proposed. A hybrid approach coupling 
MobileNet’s onboard spatial pattern extraction to LSTM’s 
temporal dependency analysis in video sequences, affords a 
robust manipulation identification system based on deepfake.  
Fig. 1. System Architecture 
 
A. Data Collection and Preprocessing 
A huge and diverse set of real and deepfake media was 
collected in order to create a robust, versatile model. The 
dataset encompasses different methods of deepfake 
generation and it generalizes them over various manipulation 
techniques. The datasets utilized in this work are taken from 
the Kaggle free dataset library called the [19] Deepfake 
Detection Challenge dataset and Deep Fake Detection (DFD) 
Entire Original Dataset. 
1. Data Augmentation: Transformation of data set 
such as rotation, flipping, addition of noise, 
cropping for enhancing dataset diversity to create a 
real world dataset and make them robust. 
2. Frame Extraction and Normalization:  Resizing 
frames of videos to have consistent resolution and 
fix pixel bit values to avoid skewing the data. 
3. Balanced Dataset:  Preventing bias by ensuring 
repeated distribution of real versus fake data over 
multiple deepfake techniques. 
Preprocessing with these techniques provides the same 
consistent, well-structured input dataset that is critical for 
training models and detecting.

B. Feature Extraction with MobileNet 
MobileNet, a lightweight CNN architecture, during 
individual frames, it uses MobileNet V3 to efficiently extract 
spatial features. For videos, extract frames from video at 
optimal intervals using OpenCV. It then makes a prediction 
on whether the common cues (e.g. texture, facial alignment, 
lighting variation) imply manipulation. Key improvements 
include: 
1. Pre-Trained Weights:  Enhanced feature extraction 
using transfer learning using weights pre trained on 
ImageNet dataset. 
2. Batch Normalization: However, by using batch 
normalization layers, it can be stabilized as training 
and accelerate convergence. 
3. Real-Time 
Capability: 
Enabling 
real 
time 
applications with same detection precision with 
MobileNet V3’s fast processing speed. 
These extracted spatial features constitute the input for 
subsequent temporal analysis used to detect inconsistencies 
in video sequences. 
C. Temporal Analysis with LSTM 
An LSTM network is used based on the MobileNet V3 
spatial features extracted from video frames to analyze the 
sequential feature relationships. It is shown that LSTM is able 
to capture temporal patterns such as motion and expression 
inconsistency that are difficult to replicate in the deepfakes 
videos. The enhancements include: 
1. Hyperparameter Tuning:  Improving the temporal 
dependent analysis via optimization of the number 
of LSTM units, dropout rates and activation 
functions. 
2. Temporal 
Loss 
Function: 
Motivation 
for 
introducing a loss function for emphasizing 
sequential consistency and improving temporal 
feature learning. 
This integration allows addressing the manipulation detection 
over time, which is typically ignored with single shot 
analysis. 
D. Hybrid Model Training 
Optimization abilities towards spatial and temporal 
pattern of detection are used to train a MobileNet-LSTM 
hybrid model, over the preprocessed dataset. At the end, the 
classification layer uses the softmax function this gives the 
idea of possibilities of if the image or a video is created using 
the deepfake technology or not [19]. 
Training involves: 
1. Learning Rate Scheduling:  Convergence efficiently 
using 
ReduceLROnPlateau 
for 
learning 
rate 
adjustment dynamically. 
2. Adversarial Training:  To improve model robustness 
against sophisticated deepfake techniques, it can 
expose the model to adversarial examples. 
3. Combined Loss Functions:  A combination of binary 
cross entropy loss for classification and temporal loss 
for sequential analysis are used. 
The hybrid model is intended to be elastic to multiple 
deepfake approaches, both robust and versatile in detecting. 
E. User Interface Development 
For practical applications a user-friendly interface is 
developed which permits a user to upload images or video or 
files to be analyzed.  Key features include: 
1. Real-Time Detection:  Real-time deepfake analysis 
is supported for the interface displaying a detection 
score as well as visual explanations of identified 
anomalies. 
2. Transparency and Usability:  Media verification 
by identifying and highlighting manipulated regions 
within media targeting user trust and providing 
actionable insights for the media verifier. 
As for the interface, it is designed to be accessible for non-
technical users which makes it apt at application areas, 
journalism, social media monitoring, law enforcement, etc. 
F. Model Evaluation and Optimization 
Metrics like accuracy, precision, recall and F1 score are 
used to evaluate trained models so that it’s reliable.  Key steps 
include: 
1. Performance 
Validation: 
Adaptability 
and 
robustness on unseen deepfake techniques. 
2. Hyperparameter 
Fine-Tuning: 
Change 
of 
MobileNet V3 and LSTM parameters to achieve 
high performance. 
3. Processing Speed Optimization: Architectural 
adjustment to achieve real time requirement for 
detection speed. 
The optimizations made sure that the model remains stable 
and efficient in different operational situations. 
G. Deployment and Real-World Testing 
The model can be optimized for deployment on platforms 
from social media to news agencies and content verification 
services.  Real-world testing involves: 
1. Diverse Media Sources: Running the model on 
media from different sources to validate its accuracy 
and speed under varied conditions. 
2. User Feedback Integration: Tinkering the model 
and interface to get better usability and performance 
through feedback.

V. CONCLUSION  
This work introduces a hybrid MobileNet-LSTM model 
resulting in a robust and effective solution for deepfake 
detection, leveraging the joint usage of spatial and temporal 
analysis. By virtue of the efficient extraction of fine grained 
spatial features by the MobileNet component and temporal 
patterns on sequential frames by the LSTM network, 
manipulations in the images as well as video are detected. The 
experimental results verify the superiority of the proposed 
model compared to standalone architectures and traditional 
CNN based methods that result in superior accuracy, 
precision, recall and F1-score. Finally, accuracy of the 
MobileNet-LSTM model is 91.8%, which is robust and 
adaptable to different deep fake techniques. The model 
achieves a precision of 89.4% and recall of 90.5%, with high 
reliability in terms of detecting manipulated content and low 
rate of false positives and negatives. This results in a balanced 
F1-score of 89.9% which demonstrates that it generalizes well 
across all sorts of real world testing scenarios rendering it well 
suited to be used as part of numerous applications. The 
proposed model is lightweight and enables real time detection, 
supporting real time deepfake media detection requiring 
timely and accurate detection. This capability makes social 
media monitoring, digital forensics, journalism and public 
safety practical applications of this capability. The user-
friendly interface also improves usability by providing non-
technical users an intuitive way to get detection results and 
visual explanations of detected anomalies helping build trust 
and transparency for business users. Overall, the proposed 
hybrid MobileNet-LSTM model makes the contributions of 
removing the spatial and temporal limitations of deepfake 
detection systems by using spatial analysis and temporal 
analysis. And owing to its real time capability, high 
performance and user friendly design, it is an appropriate tool 
to combat the persistent problem of deepfake media. Later 
works may be aimed at incorporating multimodal inputs, e.g. 
audio and textual analysis, to further enhance detection 
accuracy and flexibility in dynamic digital environments
