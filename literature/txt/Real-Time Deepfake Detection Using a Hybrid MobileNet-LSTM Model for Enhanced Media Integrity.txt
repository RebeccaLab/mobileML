Abstract: The proliferation of deepfake media stirred grave doubts over media 
authenticity threatening digital trust and social coherence. This paper proposes a 
new technique of deepfake detection from images and videos through the use of 
a hybrid of MobileNet spatial feature extraction and Long Short Term Memory 
(LSTM) networks for the analysis of temporal patterns. The proposed system 
ensures efficient real-time detection by achieving high classification accuracy 
while keeping computational efficiency. The model integrates Explainable AI 
(XAI) techniques such as Grad-CAM heatmaps to provide visual interpretation 
of the detected anomalies which can be measured using data from different the 
system is optimized for deployment on edge devices and offers scalability across 
platforms like social media news agencies and law enforcement of large scale 
applications. Future enhancements include extending system capabilities to mul-
ti-modal deepfake detection and continuous learning for evolving threats. 
Keywords: Deepfake detection, MobileNet, LSTM, real-time analysis, Ex-
plainable AI, Grad-CAM, edge devices, scalable systems. 
1. INTRODUCTION 
The express development of deepfake technology has increased the challenges of 
distinguishing between genuine and faked media. Deepfakes involve virtual media in 
which a face is replaced or swapped. They have been utilized for dozens of illegal 
activities from disinformation and defamation to identity theft. Faked videos and im-
ages present overwhelming challenges to digital media integrity. 
Sophisticated deepfake detection methods typically utilize deep learning-based tech-
nique namely Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction.

Most of the frameworks, however, do not take into account temporal inconsistency in

of Grad-CAM heatmaps, among others, to provide anomalies that are detected to 
enhance transparency of detection. 
2. LITERATURE REVIEW 
Aarthe et al. [1] proposed an hybrid deep learning model that incorporates convolu-
tional layers for spatial processing and fully connected layers for classification. Their 
method targeted accuracy without being tested under real-time constraints. Saikia et 
al. [2] proposed a CNN-LSTM model that employs optical flow to get temporal in-
consistencies, proving to be effective for detection from videos but being computa-
tionally costly. Likewise, Tipper et al. [8] proved the CNN-LSTM pair for video 
deepfake detection, highlighting the need for temporal dynamics. Agnihotri [3] dis-
cussed CNN-based architectures and concluded that deeper networks are precise but 
not suitable for real-time detection because they have increased latency. Raza et al. 
[4] employed a CNN-based approach to image-level deepfake detection with better 
performance on static datasets but not temporal manipulation. Qazi and Ahmed [5] 
utilized transfer learning in combination with ensemble methods to enhance generali-
zability across domains, whereas Helode et al. [6] explored hybrid models that com-
bine machine learning and deep learning and leverage both learned and manually 
designed features, thereby exhibiting improved robustness. Sundaram et al. [7] uti-
lized deep learning models to verify video, again emphasizing the significance of 
temporal consistency in identifying tampered content. Singh et al. [10] utilized an 
EfficientNet model enhanced with self-attention mechanisms that exhibited improved 
performance but at the cost of increased complexity. Rajeev and Raviraj [9] contrast-
ed the performance of various deep learning models, and MobileNet and other light-
weight CNNs are promising candidates for real-time detection. Some detailed reviews 
have also provided a robust foundation for the understanding of the scope of tech-
niques. Heidari et al. [12] and Rana et al. [16] presented systematic reviews on trends 
in deepfake detection, such as traditional, deep learning, and hybrid approaches. 
Zhang [17] and Patel et al. [18] discussed generation approaches as well as detection 
challenges, referring to the necessity for generalizable and scalable approaches. Sev-
eral datasets have prompted research into deepfakes. Dolhansky et al. [11] introduced 
the DeepFake Detection Challenge (DFDC) dataset, a very diverse and popular 
benchmark video dataset. Zhao et al. [13] proposed a self-consistency detection 
framework based on internal feature alignment for distinguishing between real and 
fake. Zi et al. [14] offered the WildDeepfake dataset, simulating real-world distortions

poral analysis. MobileNet effectively identifies facial anomalies and visual inconsist-
encies at the frame level, while the LSTM network captures temporal dependencies 
and detects irregularities in movement patterns over time, especially for video data. 
The model learns to distinguish true and fake media with spatial feature analysis and

ogy allowed the development of very realistic deep-fake media. This creates tremen-

artifacts, and as a result, the information landscape becomes plagued by increased 
misinformation, political manipulation, and social trust issues. The detection models 
employed are also plagued by overfitting and demand huge, heterogeneous datasets 
to train them effectively, which is hindered by considerations regarding privacy and 
data availability. The ineffectiveness of the current method of detection makes it 
increasingly simple to spread malicious content making it more important to create 
stronger and better models. The use of hybrid models integrating CNNs, RNNs and 
attention mechanisms such as the MobileNet-LSTM model discussed here presents 
potential solutions by increasing detection precision and model robustness. 
3.2 
Benefits of Hybrid Deepfake Detection Systems 
In comparison to baseline models hybrid deepfake detection systems employing both 
LSTM and MobileNet architectures have been found to demonstrate a remarkable 
improvement in detection accuracy . By employing both spatial and temporal analysis 
the system is able to detect not just obvious visual anomalies like inconsistent facial 
features but also speech patterns eye gaze and head movement—features that typical 
CNN-based models tend to overlook. The technique maximizes resource usage as it 
minimizes the need for extensive training datasets while maximizing model perfor-
mance across different deepfake datasets. The use of attention mechanisms, e.g., Grad-
CAM, increases the system's interpretability, hence the transparency in the model's 
decision-making process and building trust in its outcomes. The hybrid model's ability 
to identify fine-grained manipulation artifacts makes it a crucial part of the war against 
the dissemination of deepfakes on online media.

3.3 
Concerns in Deepfake Detection Using AI and IoT 
Though deepfake detection models have been demonstrated to have high potential, 
the integration of such models with real-time tracking using IoT devices is susceptible 
to various challenges. High development costs of AI models, such as the requirement 
of high-performance computation resources and a large dataset, restrict the use of 
such solutions. Moreover, deployment of such models on edge devices are susceptible 
to complexities such as scalability/real-time processing and power constraints. Data 
security is also a challenge as deepfake detection systems are highly dependent on 
internet-based devices used for data collection and transmission so that they can 
therefore be misused. Moreover, the deepfake detection models need to be robust for 
functioning under varied conditions, such as varying lighting conditions or camera 
angles, which tend to degrade model performance. AI-IoT integration is highly com-
plex and therefore demands interdisciplinary interaction to solve the problem of mod-
el performance, installation, and security. 
4. METHODOLOGY 
4.1 
Overview 
Although 3D CNNs and Transformers have been showing potential in temporal mod-
eling, they were used with LSTM due to the performance vs. cost advantage of 
LSTM, particularly for real-time and edge-device applications. 3D CNNs consume 
much more GPU memory and computation, and Transformer-based models, although 
precise, are plagued with latency and scalability challenges. LSTM provides robust 
sequence modeling with lower resource requirements, which is consistent with the 
system design requirements. Deepfake detection system is developed with the main 
objective of efficiently and accurately identifying media content that has been manip-
ulated or forged in any manner. Advanced analysis of spatial and temporal features is 
used in this system, which is coupled with a hybrid model based on MobileNet and 
LSTM architecture. In particular, MobileNet is used as a light-weight convolutional 
neural network, which is used for extracting spatial features from a single frame of a 
video. In contrast, the LSTM module is pivotal in examining the sequential features of 
the frames, thus facilitating the detection of any temporal inconsistencies that could 
reflect manipulation. Inceptive integration of these cutting-edge architectures gives 
the system the capability to identify deepfake videos in real time, which is a huge leap 
compared to current approaches that have a tendency to examine solely spatial fea-
tures or temporal features in isolation.

4.2 
Proposed Design 
Grad-CAM is applied to the output obtained from the final convolutional layer of the 
MobileNet model. The heatmaps show regions of facial abnormalities (e.g., fuzzy 
boundaries and irregular eye movement) that led to the classification result. The vis-
ualizations are made available to the end user via the interface, ensuring transparency 
and enabling human verification of the system's conclusion. The system has been 
designed on the basis of a sequential pipeline to specifically process incoming video 
inputs and then determine whether each of the inputs is real or fake. The first step 
includes a preprocessing phase for the videos where the frames are sampled at an 
equal interval such that the data being processed becomes standardized. After the 
frames are collected, they are normalized and resized to ensure uniformity in shape 
prior to being processed with the MobileNet model tasked with extracting different 
features from the frames. The next step includes taking the extracted spatial features 
and passing them into the LSTM network tasked with detecting and analyzing the 
temporal relationships that exist between different frames in the process of the video. 
Thereafter, the features that have been concatenated together subsequently passed 
into fully connected layers, giving the final output of the classification of the content 
of the video. Furthermore, the model incorporates explainable AI mechanisms such 
as Grad-CAM, where the manipulated regions in the process of the video frames are 
visualized, giving users a better idea of the manner in which the AI made its specific 
decision in relation to the realness of the video.

4.3      Methodology 
The model was trained and evaluated on a combination of three established datasets, 
i.e., FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF (v2), and the DeepFake Detection Challenge Dataset 
(DFDC). The datasets provide a variety of deepfake creation techniques and source 
video samples, which the model is able to generalize across many manipulation tech-
niques. In the preprocessing pipeline, several steps are taken to provide high-quality 
input to the model; this includes extracting frames from videos at a constant and con-
sistent rate to provide uniformity. Also, there is a very crucial step of resizing and 
normalizing these images to provide uniformity in dimensions and format. Also, a 
series of data augmentation operations are used, such as the addition of Gaussian 
noise, horizontal flipping of images, and random cropping techniques, all of which are 
used to boost the model's ability to generalize. Also, optical flow analysis is a very 
crucial part of this process by being used to identify motion inconsistencies that can 
be indicative of manipulation characteristic of deepfakes. 
During the training process, the spatial and temporal features that are learned are very 
optimized, which is achieved using cross-entropy loss together with a number of 
components of the Adam optimizer. To enhance detection performance while, simul-
taneously, preventing the risk of overfitting, both the batch size and learning rate are 
uniquely determined through a process of hyperparameter optimization. This cautious 
process has resulted in an efficient model evaluation process that not only encom-
passes the training process but also the validation and test sets of the concerned da-
taset. Generalization to unseen instances of deepfake content is also enhanced using 
regularization techniques, including using dropout layers in the LSTM module, as 
explained in source. 
The whole system is efficiently hosted on a cloud server, which has been specially 
optimized to support smooth real-time video analysis. The system deployment is a set 
of containerized microservices that are tasked with performing the important task of 
model inference, which in turn makes the system highly scalable and efficient on a 
range of different devices. Web and mobile clients can directly communicate with the 
detection model using a well-defined REST API, thus allowing users to send videos 
that need deepfake verification. To allow the overall user experience to be highly 
improved, different real-time feedback mechanisms, such as confidence scores and 
visual explanations, have been easily exposed to users. With the addition of GPU 
acceleration, the system shows low latency, which in turn makes the system highly 
appropriate for large-scale real-time applications that need instant processing and 
feedback.

5. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 
5.1 
Performance Evaluation 
The performance measures used are accuracy (96.3%), precision (95.7%), recall 
(96.8%), F1-score (96.2%), and ROC-AUC (0.981). These measures all confirm the 
balance of the model in detecting fraudulent and authentic videos, thereby confirming 
their reliability in real applications. The precision and recall values reflect the power 
of the model to classify deepfake and real videos properly along with a balanced F1-
score displaying its overall power while inference time is optimized bringing an aver-
age of less than 500ms per frame. 
5.2 
Comparative Analysis 
For comparative analysis, our suggested MobileNet-LSTM model was compared with 
top-performing models like XceptionNet, EfficientNet-B0, and MesoNet. Although 
XceptionNet and EfficientNet gave an accuracy of 95.1% and 94.6%, respectively, 
our suggested model gave an accuracy of 96.3% but at a much lesser computational 
cost with the possibility of real-time inference. Lightweight MesoNet provided quick-
er processing but at a lower accuracy of 90.4%. 
5.3 
Deployment 
The deepfake detection system is hosted on a cloud server and attached with a user-
friendly web and mobile application. Users can upload videos to get the proper and 
timely checking of its authenticity. The system is deploying for scalability to handle 
multiple requests at a time with low-latency processing. Explainable AI features such 
as Grad-CAM visualizations will also provide insights on why a video is classified as 
deepfake, further building user trust. These usability enhancements make it accessible 
to journalists, content creators, or cybersecurity experts looking for a trustworthy 
media verification tool.

6. DISCUSSION 
Ethical concerns are highest in detecting deepfakes. Mislabeling genuine content as 
deepfake can damage reputations and spread misinformation. We incorporate a 
threshold tuning mechanism into our system to avoid such risks, and confidence 
scores are returned to users to allow them to make their own judgments. To avoid 
abuse, like authoritarian abuse or biased moderation, we suggest that the system be 
integrated with open human review layers and publicly accessible audit logs. The 
experimental outcomes show that exploiting spatial and temporal features for deep-
fake detection is advantageous. Application of this technique is far superior to static 
image-based detection methods, which do not consider motion inconsistencies and are 
therefore susceptible to more advanced deepfake techniques. Adding Explainable AI 
also gives interpretability to this model, so that users can see the rationale behind 
classifying a particular video as a fake. The biggest challenge is to come up with im-
proved defenses against adversarial deepfake attacks, where the model was trained 
not to be detected. Future research efforts in this area will focus on strengthening the 
system's robustness against attacks, reducing the rate of false positives, and improving 
performance in particular in the context of edge devices that have limited computa-
tional capabilities. 
7. CONCLUSION 
The deepfake detection model that is developed utilizes MobileNet and LSTM and is 
highly accurate and real-time. The model delivers results that are interpretable, trans-
parent and win the user's trust. The experimental results validate that the model is 
robust and delivers improved performance and computational efficiency for deploy-
ment at a large scale compared to the other models. The cloud deployment also im-
proves the scalability of the model, and thus the accessibility becomes smoother on 
the web and mobile platforms. The utilization of an optimized processing pipeline and 
a lightweight architecture renders the system accessible to journalists, media outlets 
and cybersecurity professionals. Federated learning is being researched actively as the 
next stage of development. The method enables edge devices to learn from non-
centralized data to share, thus maintaining user data privacy and enhancing model 
diversity and resistance to new generative processes. 
A real-time feedback system not only contributes significantly to enhancing user ex-
perience but also makes the deepfake verification process easier and more efficient. 
Future work in this direction will be aimed at enhancing the ability of the model to 
generalize well under the presence of adversarial conditions and its detection ability 
particularly for new and unseen generative models. In addition, the incorporation of 
federated learning mechanisms into the system can provide an added layer of privacy 
while at the same time increasing the diversity in the dataset used. This work is at the
