Abstract—The rapid advancement of deep learning has made
it possible for the generation of hyper-realistic synthetic me-
dia, commonly known as deepfakes, which pose significant
threats to privacy, security, and digital trust. Most existing
detection approaches available are computationally expensive
and unsuitable for real-time use on mobile platforms. This
paper presents UnMask, which is a lightweight and scalable
multi-modal real-time deepfake detection application capable of
processing image, audio, and video inputs with high accuracy.
The proposed framework integrates optimized deep learning
models such as DenseNet121 for image classification, a CNN-
based architecture for audio analysis, and an EfficientNet-B0
with BiLSTM and attention mechanism for video detection into
a FastAPI-powered backend. A corresponding lightweight mobile
application, ”UnMask,” was developed using dart programming
language and Flutter, which enables both prerecorded and live
media verification using those models. Companion website was
also build for the users to get to know about the app’s features
and download the app on mobile devices. Experimental results
demonstrate strong performance, achieving accuracy scores of
99% for images and audio and 73% for video detection on
benchmark datasets. These findings highlight the effectiveness of
the proposed approach and its potential for real-time deployment
on resource-constrained mobile devices to enhance digital media
integrity.
Index Terms—Deepfake Detection, Multi-Modal System, Mo-
bile Application, Deep Learning, CNN.
I. INTRODUCTION
The fast progress of artificial intelligence (AI) and deep
learning has allowed the development of hyper-realistic syn-
thetic media, which are also referred to as deepfakes. These
fake images, produced mainly by generative adversarial net-
works (GANs) and analogous structures, are capable of con-
vincingly emulating human faces, voices and even more com-
plex gestures [1]. Although these technologies can be applied
legitimately in the entertainment, education and online com-
munication sectors, they have also created significant threats to
privacy, security and confidence to the masses. Deepfakes are
now commonly used in disinformation campaigns, monetary
fraud and identity theft, as well as cyber harassment, which
threatens digital integrity and social stability [2]–[4].
These risks are increased by the increased dependency on
mobile devices to consume content. Regardless of the advances
in research, it is important to note that the majority of the
current detection systems are still resource-intensive, cloud-
based or limited to forensic and institutional applications,
which makes them inappropriate to be deployed on smart-
phones [5], [6]. This poses a vital weakness because regular
users who are the most vulnerable to deepfake content do not
have readily available, real-time, verification tools. Besides,
the complexity of multi-modal deepfakes, which can change
audio, video and image streams at the same time, renders
the old unimodal detection strategies ineffective [1], [7]. It
is necessary to resolve these issues using not only the correct
models of detection, but also the lightweight model, scalable,
and easy to use by the non-technical population.
To resolve this challenges we have designed and developed
UnMask, a lightweight and scalable multi-modal deepfake de-
tection system for mobile deployment. The main contributions
are as follows: (1) optimized deep learning models for image,
audio, and video deepfake detection with low computational
overhead; (2) integration of these models into a FastAPI back-
end for real-time inference on resource-constrained devices;
(3) development of a user-friendly mobile application and
companion website for broad accessibility; and (4) extensive
experimental evaluation demonstrating high accuracy and gen-
eralizability across benchmark datasets.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
Initial attempts at detecting a deepfake image were based
on the human eye or rule-based detection, which detected
artifacts, including unnatural lighting, irregular blinking, and
inaccurate lip-synchronization [8]. Nonetheless, with the matu-
ration of deepfake generation approaches, such hand methods
have become more and more ineffective [9]. Deep learning
has since become the paradigm. Convolutional Neural Net-
works (CNNs) are best at identifying visual discrepancies in
images and video frames, whereas Recurrent Neural Networks
(RNNs), especially Long Short-Term Memory(LSTM) models
show the ability to model temporal relationships in audio
and video streams [10]. Improved robustness is achieved
by hybrid CNN-RNN methods which are a combination of
spatial and temporal analysis [11]. More recent developments
also involve attention mechanisms, which improve detection
through specializing in small areas of manipulation with less
computational overhead, which are also potentially useful in
mobile applications [12], [13].
Adversarial training has contributed to robustness, exposing
detection models to adversarially generated manipulations
during training, and thus improving generalization to unseen
forgeries [14], [15]. Also, multi-modal detection systems that
share the analysis of visual, auditory, and temporal information
have been effective in detecting cross-modal anomalies like
discrepant lip movements and speech timing [14], [16].
Lightweight optimization methods, including model prun-
ing, quantization, and knowledge distillation have become
essential enablers of real-time deployment because of the con-
straints of mobile platforms [10], [17]. A range of optimized
architectures, such as InceptionResNetV2, ResNet-50 and the
hybrid CNN-LSTM models, have demonstrated detection rates
of over 95% on test datasets, including FaceForensics++,
Celeb-DF, and the Deepfake Detection Challenge (DFDC)
[18]–[20]. There are additional new techniques, including
ensemble learning based on biological signals [21], detection
of lighting inconsistencies [18], and watermarking schemes
[22], which enlarge the detection space. Despite these advance-
ments, challenges remain in dataset diversity, cross-dataset
generalization, and deployment on resource-constrained mo-
bile devices. Most existing methods are either modality-
specific or computationally heavy, leaving a gap in lightweight,
real-time, multi-modal detection systems optimized for mobile
platforms. This research seeks to bridge that gap by developing
Unmask, a scalable, mobile-based solution that integrates deep
learning models with a user-centric design to strengthen digital
media integrity in the face of synthetic manipulation.
III. METHODOLOGY
This section outlines the datasets, preprocessing techniques,
model architectures, backend integration, mobile app imple-
mentation, and website development, detailing the complete
pipeline of the proposed multi-modal deepfake detection sys-
tem. Fig. 1 shows the workflow diagram of our Unmask mobile
application.
A. Dataset acquisition and preprocessing
For image deepfake detection, we used a dataset of 140k
real and fake faces. This dataset consists of 70,000 fake
faces selected from 1 million synthetic faces produced by
StyleGAN which was supplied by Bojan, and also 70000
real faces taken from the Flickr-Faces-HQ (FFHQ) dataset
that was gathered by Nvidia. Training images were resized
to 256×256, randomly flipped for augmentation, converted to
tensors, and normalized using ImageNet statistics. Validation
and test sets followed the same pipeline without flipping. Data
were organized with ImageFolder and loaded via PyTorch
DataLoader with shuffling (training only) and a batch size of
64 for efficient processing.
We used an open-source dataset “In the wild” for training
our audio deepfake detection model. The dataset basically
consist of audio deepfakes for a set of politicians and other
public figures, collected from publicly available sources such
as social networks and video streaming platforms. For the
number of 58 celebrities and politicians, the dataset has a
collection of 20.8 hours of real and 17.2 hours of fake audio.
It consists of 19963 real audio samples and 11816 fake audio
samples of short durations. All input audio samples were
standardized to a sampling rate of 22.05 kHz with a fixed
duration of 2 seconds. To ensure uniformity, shorter clips were
zero-padded while longer clips were trimmed. Each waveform
x(t) was transformed into a Mel-spectrogram representation
using the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), defined as:

Video data were collected from the FaceForensics++
dataset, excluding the official test split to prevent leakage. An

batch normalization, dropout, and a final sigmoid output neu-

embeddings into a binary output space, distinguishing between
real and fake faces.

C. FastAPI-Based Multi-Modal Backend
The UnMask system employs a FastAPI-based backend to
support multi-modal deepfake detection on images, audio, and
video. The service handles API requests, preprocesses inputs,
forwards them to pretrained models, and returns predictions
in real time. Hardware optimization enables automatic device
selection (CUDA, MPS, or CPU), ensuring efficiency across
platforms. The backend is started with the Uvicorn ASGI
server to allow access to the device’s frontend. It is basically
configured to bind to all interfaces (0.0.0.0) on port 8082. It
helps mobile and other devices out on the same local network
to access the backend services by reaching the IP address of
the host machine. Fig. 3 shows the API endpoints for client
uploads (image, audio, video).

D. Mobile App Development
The ’Unmask’ app aims to provide a user-friendly and
powerful tool for detecting deepfakes, which enables users
to conduct media analysis on their mobile phones. We used
dart programming language and flutter-an open-source frame-
work made by Google, for building the app. Simulation and
coding was done on Android Studio. We used Supabase as
the backend-as-a-service (BaaS) platform that handles user
authentication, session management, and secure data storage
on the backend of our app.
After uploading an image, audio, or video, the app performs
rapid analysis and provides an authenticity prediction with a
confidence score. This real-time feedback is crucial for users
requiring immediate results. Beyond pre-recorded media, the
app also supports live capture of photos, videos, and audio
recordings, enabling on-the-spot verification. Fig. 4 shows the
user interface of the UnMask application.

V. CONCLUSION
This work introduced UnMask, a practical and scalable
multi-modal deepfake detection system tailored for real-time
use on mobile platforms. By combining lightweight deep
learning models for image, audio, and video analysis with
an efficient FastAPI backend and user-friendly interfaces, the
system bridges the gap between high-performance detection
and accessibility for everyday users. Experimental results re-
vealed near-perfect accuracy for image and audio detection and
competitive performance for video classification, highlighting
both the strengths and areas for refinement. Looking ahead,
we aim to further enhance the robustness of video detection
by addressing temporal inconsistencies and improving cross-
dataset generalization. Additionally, future developments will
focus on expanding support for newly emerging deepfake
modalities while keeping the system lightweight and user-
centric. We believe that UnMask provides a strong step
toward safeguarding digital trust and empowering users with
accessible tools to counteract synthetic media threats.
