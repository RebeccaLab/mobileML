Abstract—The advancement of open-source frameworks and
user-friendly manipulation applications has accelerated the
spread of deep fakes. In this study, we proposed optimal features
assisted with a dual attention (DA) network strategy to combat
this proliferation in consumer imaging devices. We employed
EfﬁcientNetV2 (ENV2) as the primary feature extractor, initially
utilizing its pre-trained weights from the ImageNet dataset
while keeping its layers frozen to leverage their rich feature
extraction capabilities. We enhance this base model with a
DA module that integrates the Convolutional Block Attention
Module (CBAM), which utilizes both channel attention (CA) and
spatial attention (SA) mechanisms to improve feature represen-
tation. CA dynamically adjusts channel-wise feature responses to
capture interdependencies between channels, thereby improving
feature discrimination. SA allows the network to focus on
important regions within feature maps, enhancing localization
and reducing noise. The features are assisted in multi-stages
of the network with residual fashion to focus on discriminative
visual information. During ﬁne-tuning, we unfreeze the deeper
layers of ENV2 for further reﬁnement of learned features to
better suit the deepfake dataset. We employed a targeted ﬁne-
tuning approach, unfreezing speciﬁc layers and applying iterative
adjustments to optimize performance, providing valuable insights
into countering the growing use of synthetic media in consumer
imaging. To validate our network, we conducted comprehensive
experiments on four benchmark datasets: FaceForensics++, the
World Leaders, Celeb-DF, and DFDC. As a result, our network
achieved superior performance compared to existing bench-
marks and state-of-the-art approaches, offering a promising
solution for robust deepfake detection (DD) in consumer imaging
technologies.

I. INTRODUCTION
C
ONSUMER Electronics, especially smartphones and
cameras, have transformed media creation and sharing.
While inexpensive, high-quality CE has made content creation
more accessible [1], it has also contributed to the development
of deepfake technology, raising questions about authenticity.
Deepfakes pose a widespread hazard, affecting the privacy of
public and private people, especially celebrities, politicians,
high-security personnel, and other public ﬁgures. This spreads
false information and scams, compromising personal security,
damaging reputations, and undermining public trust in media.
Malicious actors exploit these technologies for misinforma-
tion, which comes under cyber crime [2]. Hence, face privacy
preservation is often considered a burning issue [3]. To tackle
this issue, strong deepfake detection (DD) systems are required
to be integrated into consumer devices. There is an urgent need
to detect fake content to safeguard security and conﬁdence in
the use of digital media [4]. CE companies are more interested
in addressing challenges posed by deepfake technology to
retain their brand reputation and customer trust [5].
The emergence of deepfake technology has resulted in
situations where facial features in pictures and videos have
been transformed [6], [7]. This has led to a decrease in trust
and safety in digital media. Deepfake technology signiﬁ-
cantly affects consumer appearance as consumers increasingly
depend on visual media for information, entertainment, and
communication [8]. Therefore, it is crucial, especially in
today’s digital era, to develop strong networks that are capable
of recognizing forgeries to tackle the increasing challenges
related to security and trust in consumer imaging technology.
Most of the attempted methods currently in use focus on
particular varieties of deepfakes. For instance, [9] suggested a
technique for spotting lip-syncing deepfakes. Their approach
took advantage of the discrepancies between a phoneme (spo-
ken word) and a viseme (mouth form). Another attempt [10]
proposed a data augmentation-based method, Face-Cutout.
As from the name, they cut the irrelevant facial landmark
information and then selected only relevant features and parts
of video frames. To extract facial landmark features, the
study [11] employed the open-source toolbox OpenFace2.
These generated features were combined with an action unit to
train a binary SVM for DD. The work [9] proposed the end-to-
end system MesoNet to identify phony Face2Face videos and

A. Limitations of the Related Literature
The limitations of existing studies in the domain highlight
the need for feasible techniques for deployment on consumer
devices. One of the signiﬁcant limitations of most existing
methods is the inadequate capacity to capture complex features
precisely. This limitation is often based on the reliance
on the direct utilization of intermediate feature extractors
without proper empirical validation at the early stages of the
networks. Moreover, the lack of optimal features is unable
to be learned with shallow layered networks to grasp the
important patterns before the ultimate prediction. Attention-
based techniques have shown promise in enhancing feature
representation and discriminability. However, their utilization
in existing architectures is often technically unsystematic, with
less representative features. As a result, the learned features
may not be effectively reﬁned and optimized. This can result
in less discriminative and less informative feature representa-
tions, hindering the model’s ability to detect DD accurately.
Moreover, the lack of generalized optimization methods for
the utilized modules can impact the overall effectiveness of
the network, especially when faced with evolving synthetic
media manipulation methods. These limitations emphasize the
immediate need for feasible solutions that directly tackle the
challenges mentioned above, thereby enabling the design of a
more effective network.

II. RELATED LITERATURE
Various approaches have been proposed to tackle the DD
challenge, leveraging both traditional and advanced techniques
in machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) [15].
To address the unique demands of DD, researchers have
explored many methodologies for DD [16]. For instance,
the work [11] used videos of individuals to extract facial
expressions and head movements from hours-long videos.
They further extracted features from a 10-second clip, as frame
by frame and 190-D features were found by them. SVM
was used to classify the 10-second clip as being consistent
or not with the learned mannerisms of an individual. The
attempted technique requires much effort to propose a model
for each, and hand-crafted features might not be optimal,
highlighting the complexity inherent in securing CE against
deepfake threats. Another framework called FakeCatcher was
proposed [17] for DD using signals from three regions of the
face in actual videos. The method presented a promising way
for DD, especially in CE devices, where user interaction is
vital. Moreover, the study [18] proposed a method that used
CNN to extract features from video frames and then feed
them to LSTM to predict sequential patterns. However, the
technique was evaluated on randomly collected videos from
different websites rather than from a single standard dataset,
which may limit the test of the generalization.
The study [19] proposed a technique, Face X-ray-based
forgery detection, which used a greyscale image and used to
ﬁnd whether this image can be decomposed into the blending
of two images from various sources. Another method [9]

III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
The proposed methodology emphasizes extracting deep fea-
tures to enhance the robustness of intermediate representations.
We investigate effective modiﬁcations in CBAM to reﬁne these
features. Additionally, we introduced an optimized convolution
block with dilated convolution layers and GAP. Furthermore,
we analyze the impact of ﬁne-tuning and incremental pro-
gressive training on overall performance. As illustrated in
Figure 2, our proposed network integrates these components
to enhance accuracy and robustness, speciﬁcally designed for
CE applications.

Our proposed technique incorporates the ENV2 model as
the primary feature extractor. The model is ﬁlled with pre-
trained weights from the ImageNet dataset and set to be
non-trainable, meaning the pre-trained weights are used with-
out being updated during training. This enables the ultimate
network to take advantage of rich feature extraction ability

B. Convolutional Block Attention Module
Attention mechanisms are extensively explored in many
domains with excellent performances [29], [30], [31], [32],
[33], [34]. Taking this motivation, we integrated CBAM, which
effectively boosts the network’s ability to capture and reﬁne
features at multiple stages. CBAM receives the intermediate
features β for dynamic recalibration of CA and SA, which
progressively improves feature representation. This utilization
reduces noise information while focusing only on channels
and spatial regions. It signiﬁcantly enhances the potential
of the network to focus on various types of critical and
complex deepfakes, as highlighted in the qualitative results.
The CBAM architecture contains a dual attention scheme
where the CA module controls inter-channel functions of
features, considering each channel’s feature map. As shown in
Figure 3, CBAM architecture with CA and SA includes a GAP
layer, a maximum pooling layer, fully connected layers, and
a convolution operation. During training, convolution feature
maps of each channel do not equally represent an object.
Therefore, we incorporate both maximum and average pooling
strategies to obtain separate spatial background descriptions,
βCavg and βCmax, through average and maximum pooling

C. Optimized Dilated Convolutions Block
The customized dilated convolutions block receives the fea-
tures from CBAM and enhances features by leveraging dilated
convolution layers with varying kernel sizes. Initialized with the
input features, the block sequentially applies 3x3 convolutions
with dilation rates of 2, 4, and 8, respectively, each followed by
ReLU activation and padding to maintain spatial dimensions.
This strategic conﬁguration enables the convolutional ﬁlters to
expand their receptive ﬁelds exponentially without signiﬁcantly
increasing computational costs. As a result, the network is
able to capture both local details and global context more
effectively, which is crucial for tasks requiring ﬁne-grained
feature analysis and semantic understanding. Subsequently, the
feature maps undergo GAP to distill spatial information into
compact representations, facilitating dimensionality reduction
while retaining essential features. Additional dense layers
with ReLU activation reﬁne feature representations, promoting
nonlinear transformations that enhance discriminative power
in the extracted features. The features are then passed to the
classiﬁer layers for ultimate predictions.
D. Fine Tuning
Following the initial training phase till the 20th epoch,
the model undergoes ﬁne-tuning to optimize its performance
further for the next 10 epochs. The ENV2 model ﬁne-tunes its
deeper layers by using the ﬁrst 1000 layers as a ﬁxed feature
extractor with frozen weights. The remaining 329 layers are
set to be trainable, allowing adjustments to better suit our
dataset. This strategy allows the model to reﬁne learned
features to better ﬁt the speciﬁc weights of our large datasets.
Throughout the ﬁne-tuning phase, the model retains CBAM,
ConvNet with dilated convolutions, GAP, dense layers, and
dropout layers from the original setup. Fine-tuning continues
beyond the initial 20 epochs, spanning from epoch 21 to 30.
This phase allows for adjustments in the weights of the
deeper layers to better ﬁt our dataset while leveraging the

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
This section includes the experimental conﬁguration, the
dataset used, and the outcomes of assessing the proposed
network. The ablation study and empirical analysis of the
integration of different modules are elaborated. It also includes
a comparison with existing methods and the results of several
intermediary models.
A. Experimental Setup
We implemented our proposed network in the TensorFlow
DL framework using an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU.
We employed an Adam optimizer with a momentum of 0.9
and set the initial learning rate to 1 × 10−4. The network
was initially trained for 20 epochs with the base model
frozen. During the ﬁnal 10 epochs of the ﬁne-tuning stage,
the learning rate was reduced to accommodate the larger
model and to readapt the pre-trained weights, thereby avoiding
overﬁtting. The learning rate was further reduced by a factor
of 0.5 whenever the validation accuracy plateaued for two
consecutive epochs. We used the ReduceLROnPlateau callback
to adjust the learning rate based on validation performance.
Training images were resized to 160 × 160 and the batch size
was set to 32 as empirically validated. We employ common
metrics to evaluate our method, including Area Under the
Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC), Equal Error
Rate (EER), Accuracy (ACC), Precision, Recall, and F1-Score.
The AUC value ranges from 0 to 1, where a value closer to
1 indicates a better performance. The EER is the point on the
ROC curve where the false acceptance rate equals the false
rejection rate.

V. CONCLUSION
In this study, we introduced an innovative strategy uti-
lizing optimal features enhanced by a DA mechanism,
incorporating the CBAM to dynamically adjust channel-wise
feature responses and focus on important spatial regions.
The intermediate features are assisted and strengthened by
CBAM and customized dilated convolution block. By ﬁne-
tuning our network with progressive weight training methods,
we achieved superior performance across benchmark datasets,
including FF++, WLDR, Celeb-DF, and DFDC, outper-
forming existing state-of-the-art approaches. We conducted
intra-class validation across different classes of FF++ datasets
to evaluate the generalization capabilities of the proposed
network. There is always a need to provide an effective way
to mitigate the risks associated with media and dealings to
ensure security in digital content with greater trust. Our study
signiﬁcantly improves DDs in consumer imaging technologies.
