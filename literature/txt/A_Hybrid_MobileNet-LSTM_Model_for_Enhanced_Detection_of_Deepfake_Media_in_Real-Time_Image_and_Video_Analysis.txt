Abstract –Deepfakes, or AI-generated manipulated media, pose a 
significant threat to media integrity, privacy, and public trust. 
Detecting deepfakes effectively remains a challenge due to their 
increasingly sophisticated nature. This research addresses the issue 
of detecting both spatial and temporal inconsistencies in deepfake 
images and videos by proposing a hybrid MobileNet-LSTM model. 
The MobileNet component extracts spatial features such as texture 
anomalies and facial misalignments, while the LSTM component 
analyzes temporal dependencies to identify inconsistencies in video 
sequences, such as unnatural facial expressions or motion patterns. 
The hybrid model is trained on a large and diverse dataset of real 
and deepfake media, ensuring its adaptability to emerging deepfake 
techniques. The outcome of this research is a highly accurate, 
real-time deepfake detection system that performs with an 
accuracy of 91.8%, achieving improved precision (89.4%), recall 
(90.5%), and F1-score (89.9%) compared to existing baseline 
models. 
The model’s lightweight design ensures real-time 
performance, making it suitable for deployment in social media 
monitoring, digital forensics, and law enforcement. This work 
contributes to the ongoing efforts in combating misinformation and 
enhancing digital media authenticity by providing a scalable and 
efficient deepfake detection solution. 
 
Keywords: Media integrity, real-time detection, temporal modeling, 
image analysis, video analysis, mobilenet lstm, real time detection, 
deepfake detection. 
 
I.INTRODUCTION 
 
Advancements in artificial intelligence have produced deepfake 
technology, which is leveraged to create highly convincing 
manipulated media, such as images or videos. Deepfakes, 
initially viewed by some as a form of creative expression, have 
quickly 
become 
a 
significant 
concern 
across 
various 
fields—from politics to news media to social platforms. 
Deepfake algorithms enable the creation of synthetic content in 
which individuals appear to perform actions or make statements 
they never did, turning this medium into a powerful source of 
misinformation, a reputation destroyer, and a challenge to public 
trust in digital information. 
 
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), central to most 
deepfake generation techniques, allow the generation of a 
person’s likeness or actions with extremely high precision. 
Although deepfake generation has become more accessible and 
creates near-authentic content with or without specialized 
technical expertise, this technology continues to expand, raising 
questions about media authenticity and digital integrity. 
 
Existing deepfake detection methods focus mainly on detecting 
visual inconsistencies in individual frames, such as facial 
articulation irregularities or lighting mismatches. While these 
methods can be effective against simpler deepfakes, they 
struggle with detecting more sophisticated manipulations where 
such artifacts are masked or absent. For example, spatial-based 
models might fail to capture subtle temporal inconsistencies in 
videos, such as unnatural facial movements or motion patterns 
across 
frames. 
Another 
limitation 
is 
the 
real-time 
capability—many existing solutions are too computationally 
intensive to be used effectively in live settings, such as social 
media monitoring or real-time law enforcement applications. 
 
Thus, the research gap lies in the lack of models that can 
effectively integrate both spatial and temporal analysis for more 
accurate and real-time detection. Most methods focus on only 
one aspect—spatial features in images or temporal sequences in 
videos—while neglecting the complementary power of both. 
Additionally, there is a lack of lightweight models that can 
function in real-time on limited hardware, making deepfake 
detection less accessible for widespread deployment.

This work proposes a hybrid MobileNet-LSTM model, which

analysis of LSTM networks. This hybrid approach allows for the 
detection of both spatial anomalies (like texture and facial

II. LITERATURE SURVEY 
 
Deep learning techniques, particularly hybrid models, have been 
widely explored in video analysis, real-time recognition, and 
anomaly detection tasks. Abdullah et al. [1] introduced a 
CNN-LSTM-based model for facial expression recognition in 
videos, 
which 
combines 
the spatial feature extraction 
capabilities of CNNs with the temporal dependencies modeled 
by LSTMs, achieving significant success in video classification 
tasks. Sánchez-Caballero et al. [2] proposed a recurrent neural 
network model designed to process raw depth video data for 
real-time human action recognition, minimizing preprocessing 
overhead while improving computational efficiency. Similarly, 
Mihanpour et al. [3] integrated DB-LSTM with ResNet to 
enhance human action recognition in videos, effectively 
combining feature extraction with temporal modeling. 
 
While these models are successful in their respective domains, 
their application to deepfake detection is limited. For instance, 
Ullah et al. [4] employed CNN features combined with 
bi-directional LSTMs to detect anomalies in real-time 
surveillance networks, but their approach struggles with 
sophisticated temporal patterns inherent in deepfakes. Similarly, 
Ghadekar et al. [5] enhanced anomaly detection in dynamic 
environments like crowd scenes through ensemble learning 
techniques, but these models are not effective at detecting subtle 
facial manipulations or lip-sync errors typical in deepfake 
videos. Hashmi et al. [6] explored Conv-LSTM hybrid 
architecture for analyzing visual counterfeits, but this model 
requires fine-tuning to generalize to new and evolving deepfake 
techniques. 
 
Deepfake videos often contain subtle temporal inconsistencies, 
which are difficult to capture using traditional methods. For 
example, Yan et al. [7] developed a self-attention 3D-LSTM 
model for traffic flow forecasting, which effectively captures 
long-term spatiotemporal dependencies. However, this approach 
is computationally expensive, making it unsuitable for real-time 
applications in deepfake detection. Additionally, Jagadeesh and 
Baranidharan [8] implemented a deep learning framework for 
real-time emotion recognition in online learners, but their model 
is limited to facial expression analysis and does not address the 
more intricate manipulations found in deepfake videos. 
 
Nguyen et al. [9] proposed a multistage CNN-LSTM framework 
for fire detection in videos, demonstrating real-time capabilities, 
but their method is limited to specific event detection and does 
not generalize well to the more complex deepfake content. 
Similarly, Yin et al. [10] introduced MC-LSTM for 3D human 
action detection in healthcare applications, but it struggles to 
handle the complexity of deepfake content, especially when 
facial features are manipulated. 
 
Models such as Zhang et al. [11] and Dinaki et al. [12] focus on 
using deep learning for video emotion recognition and video 
quality forecasting, respectively. While these methods show 
promise in specific domains, they fail to provide the 
comprehensive approach needed for deepfake detection. 
Mihanpour et al. [13] combined ResNet and DB-LSTM for 
human action recognition, but their work still falls short of 
handling the complexities involved in deepfake detection, 
especially with regard to facial manipulation. 
 
Yurtsever and Eken [14] developed BabyPose, a system 
designed to decode a baby’s non-verbal communication using 
2D video-based pose estimation. While innovative, this system 
focuses on a very specific application and does not extend to 
deepfake detection, where subtle manipulation of facial and 
body features is often involved. Similarly, Yu et al. [15] used 
CNN-LSTM analysis to identify high-risk driving scenarios, but 
their model, while effective in its domain, lacks the flexibility 
needed for deepfake detection. 
 
Mittal et al. [16] explored the use of transfer learning for 
deepfake detection, which represents a promising direction for 
improving model generalization. However, their approach faces 
challenges in effectively adapting to the evolving techniques 
used by deepfake creators. Rezaei and Yazdi [17] proposed a 
framework for real-time crowd behavior recognition, but the 
focus remains on anomaly detection in surveillance videos and 
does not address deepfake content. 
 
Finally, Kang and Kim [18] utilized 1D convolutional 
autoencoders for real-time emotion classification based on PPG 
and GSR signals, demonstrating the use of physiological data for 
real-time applications. While this method offers valuable 
insights for emotion detection, it does not translate directly to 
video-based deepfake detection. Natarajan et al. [19] introduced 
an end-to-end deep learning framework for sign language 
recognition, but again, their model is domain-specific and not 
applicable to detecting manipulated video content. Yin et al. [20] 
explored the application of MC-LSTM for real-time 3D human 
action detection, which is promising for healthcare applications, 
but faces limitations in terms of adaptability to video 
manipulation in deepfakes.

B. Feature Extraction with MobileNet 
MobileNet, a lightweight CNN architecture, During individual

whether the common cues (e.g. texture, facial alignment,

video sequences. 
 
C. Temporal Analysis with LSTM 
An LSTM network is used based on the MobileNet spatial 
features extracted from video frames to analyze the sequential 
feature relationships. It is shown that LSTM is able to capture 
temporal patterns such as motion and expression inconsistency 
that are difficult to replicate in the deepfakes videos. The 
enhancements include: 
●​
Hyperparameter Tuning:  Improving the temporal 
dependent analysis via optimization of the number of 
LSTM units, dropouts rates and activation functions. 
●​
Temporal Loss Function:  Motivation for introducing 
a loss function for emphasizing sequential consistency 
and improving temporal feature learning. 
This integration allows addressing the manipulation detection 
over time, which is typically ignored with single shot analysis. 
 
D. Hybrid Model Training 
Optimization abilities towards spatial and temporal pattern of 
detection are used to train a MobileNet-LSTM hybrid model, 
over the preprocessed dataset. 
 
 Training involves: 
●​
Learning Rate Scheduling:  Convergence efficiently 
using ReduceLROnPlateau for learning rate adjustment 
dynamically. 
●​
Adversarial Training:  To improve model robustness 
against sophisticated deepfake techniques, it can expose 
the model to adversarial examples. 
●​
Combined Loss Functions:  A combination of binary 
cross entropy loss for classification and temporal loss 
for sequential analysis are used 
Our hybrid model is intended to be elastic to multiple deepfake

IV. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE 
 
The proposed system integrates lightweight spatial feature 
extraction and temporal sequence analysis to effectively detect 
deepfake media in real-time. This section provides a detailed 
overview of the architecture, describing the flow of data and 
processes from input to output. 
 
Fig 1 - System Architecture 
 
Figure 1 illustrates the system architecture of the proposed 
deepfake detection model, which integrates multiple stages for 
efficient image and video analysis. The process begins with the 
Input Module, where users can either upload images or videos or 
input URLs for analysis. After input, the data undergoes 
Preprocessing, which includes Frame Extraction, Normalization, 
and Data Augmentation to prepare the media for further 
analysis. The Feature Extraction step leverages MobileNet to 
detect spatial features, such as texture anomalies and facial 
misalignments. These spatial features are then analyzed using 
LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) for Temporal Analysis to 
identify inconsistencies across video frames. The results of this 
analysis are integrated in the Hybrid Model, which combines 
both spatial and temporal findings to provide a Confidence 
Score and determine whether the media is real or deepfake. 
Finally, the system outputs the Real-Time Detection Interface, 
which displays Visual Feedback and the Results, providing the 
user with an actionable score and insights into the detected 
anomalies.

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
 
The proposed MobileNet-LSTM hybrid model offers a 
significant advancement in deepfake detection by effectively 
combining spatial feature extraction and temporal sequence 
analysis, achieving high accuracy and real-time detection 
capabilities. The model outperforms baseline approaches, with 
an accuracy of 91.8% and an F1-score of 89.9%, demonstrating 
its robustness and versatility. However, several challenges 
remain that need to be addressed in future iterations of the 
model. Despite its high performance, the model still faces 
limitations in dealing with highly sophisticated deepfake 
techniques 
that 
may 
evolve 
as 
technology 
advances. 
Additionally, while the model is designed to be lightweight, 
further optimization is needed to make it even more efficient for 
deployment in resource-constrained environments, particularly 
for mobile devices or real-time applications with high 
computational demands. Another key challenge is ensuring the 
model's ability to adapt to new and emerging deepfake 
techniques, which can be addressed through techniques such as 
online learning and continuous model updates. Furthermore, 
expanding the model's capabilities by incorporating multimodal 
data, including audio and textual analysis, presents an exciting 
opportunity to improve detection accuracy, especially in 
complex scenarios where deepfakes manipulate multiple types 
of data simultaneously. Future work will focus on enhancing the 
model’s real-time performance, optimizing it for diverse use 
cases, and ensuring its scalability in tackling dynamic and 
evolving deepfake content. The potential for this technology to 
safeguard digital media integrity in journalism, law enforcement, 
and social media monitoring makes it a promising tool for 
tackling the growing issue of misinformation and maintaining 
public trust in digital content.
