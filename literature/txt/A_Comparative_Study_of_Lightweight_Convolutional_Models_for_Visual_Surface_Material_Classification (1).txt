ABSTRACT Surface material classification is essential for robotic perception, enabling grasp adaptation,
slip prediction, and safe manipulation. This work presents a systematic analysis of accuracy-efficiency trade-
offs among lightweight convolutional neural networks for visual-only surface material classification on the
LMT-108 database. The evaluation spans both classical and compact models, including AlexNet, ResNet-18,
ShuffleNetV2 (x0.5, x1.0, x1.5), MobileNetV2, MobileNetV3 (Small and Large), and EfficientNet (B0–B2),
all trained under an identical pipeline and assessed in terms of accuracy, parameter count, floating-point
operations (FLOPs), and inference latency. We apply a lightweight augmentation and regularization strategy
to enhance generalization on texture-rich visual data. Across all architectures, EfficientNet-B2 achieved the
highest mean Top-1 accuracy (97.8%). However, multi-objective analysis reveals that ShuffleNetV2 and
MobileNet architectures provided the best accuracy–efficiency balance, attaining ≈97% accuracy while
reducing parameters and latency by more than 70% relative to larger models. These results highlight the
suitability of modern lightweight CNNs for computationally efficient surface material classification. A
supplementary haptic fusion experiment further demonstrates that top lightweight visual backbones extend
naturally to compact multimodal perception systems.

surfaces enables autonomous systems to infer object prop-
erties, adapt grasping strategies, and navigate unstructured
environments [3], [4], [5]. Whether an autonomous entity
must determine the slipperiness of a metallic component or
distinguish carpet from tile, surface material classification
forms the foundation for numerous downstream tasks in

II. RELATED WORK
A. VISUAL TEXTURE AND MATERIAL CLASSIFICATION
CNN-based representations have largely replaced hand-
crafted texture descriptors such as LBP and co-occurrence
matrices, offering hierarchical feature extraction that general-
izes across lighting and scale variations [19], [23], [24], [25].
Early CNNs such as AlexNet and VGG were later surpassed
by residual architectures like ResNet, which improved train-
ing stability and accuracy on fine-grained material datasets.
Mandal et al. [26] demonstrated that ImageNet-pretrained
CNNs achieve high texture recognition accuracy even with-
out dataset-specific architectures, though their computational
demands remain significant. Despite these successes, system-
atic analyses of compact CNNs on material-specific datasets
such as LMT-108 remain limited [20], [24].
B. HAPTIC AND MULTIMODAL SURFACE PERCEPTION
In parallel with visual approaches, multimodal percep-
tion frameworks have leveraged tactile, acoustic, and other
sensing modalities to improve surface understanding [21],
[22], [24], [27], [28]. Substantial accuracy gains have been
demonstrated by model architectures that utilize multimodal
inputs for material classification. For example, Zheng et al.
[24] introduced FusionNet, a fully convolutional fusion
model that integrates two sub-networks: HapticNet for tactile
data, and VisualNet for image data, achieving substantially
better accuracy than single-modality performance.
Building on this, subsequent studies have explored
cross-modal feature learning [29], [30], domain adaptation,
and ensemble fusion. Liu et al. [20] proposed an optimized
ensemble approach integrating CNN, recurrent, and acous-
tic models with a feature-selection strategy, achieving good
accuracy on LMT-108. Other researchers extended multi-
modal learning with graph neural networks [31], [32], 3D
tactile point clouds [33], [34], or attention-based fusion [3],
[35]. Despite these advances, multimodal frameworks incur
higher inference latency, sensor synchronization challenges,
and hardware costs, factors that hinder deployment on small
robotic platforms [21], [22], [24]. Naturally, there is a moti-
vation to examine the upper bound of purely visual systems
before adding tactile complexity.
C. LIGHTWEIGHT EDGE-EFFICIENT CNNs
Recent lightweight architectures achieve strong accuracy–
efficiency trade-offs through structural optimizations such
as depthwise-separable convolutions and channel shuf-
fling. MobileNetV2 introduced inverted residuals [16],
ShuffleNetV2 balanced computation and memory through
channel-split design [17], and EfficientNet applied com-
pound scaling to optimize performance under fixed compute
budgets [18].
Although these networks have demonstrated competitive
performance on large-scale vision benchmarks, their com-
parative accuracy and efficiency metrics on fine-grained,
texture-centric datasets remain underexplored. Mandal et al.
[26] conducted a related comparative study on rock tex-
tures using ImageNet-pretrained CNNs and reported high
recognition accuracy, but their work did not evaluate compu-
tational metrics such as parameter count, FLOPs, or inference
latency. In contrast, the present study extends this compar-
ative perspective to lightweight CNN architectures, jointly
evaluating predictive accuracy and computational efficiency
on the LMT-108 dataset to identify optimal trade-offs for
embedded robotic perception.

This work conducts a unified evaluation of lightweight con-
volutional neural network architectures for visual surface
material classification using the LMT-108 dataset [36]. The
goal is to identify compact models capable of high accuracy
while remaining suitable for resource-constrained platforms.
The study benchmarks AlexNet, ResNet-18, ShuffleNetV2
(x1.0, x1.5), MobileNetV2, MobileNetV3 (Small and Large),
and EfficientNet-B0–B2. All models are trained under iden-
tical preprocessing, optimization, and regularization settings

A. DATASET
All experiments utilized the LMT-108 dataset [36], a com-
prehensive benchmark for surface material classification
developed by the Technical University of Munich. The dataset
contains 108 surface classes, each representing a distinct
material type. Each material was recorded under six sensing
modalities: visual, haptic, acoustic, friction, metal detection,
and reflectance.
In this study, only the visual modality was used, consisting
of RGB images captured under two illumination conditions:
with flash and without flash. Each class contains 40 images
in total: 20 for training (10 with flash, 10 without) and
20 for testing (10 with flash, 10 without), resulting in 4,320
RGB images across all 108 classes. The haptic modality was
incorporated only in the supplementary multimodal exper-
iment (Section IV-B), where accelerometer-based features
were fused with the visual embeddings to evaluate cross-
sensory generalization.
The official dataset split was used directly, with no addi-
tional resampling or repartitioning performed.

E. COMPUTATIONAL ENVIRONMENT
All experiments were conducted in PyTorch 2.3 on an
RTX 4050 GPU. Training and evaluation were performed in
Jupyter Notebook and VS Code.
Inference latency values reported in this paper correspond
to desktop-side measurements on this workstation. Latency
was measured using single-image inference (batch size =
1) with input tensors of size 3 × 224 × 224. For each
model, 100 consecutive forward passes were executed under
inference mode, and the average elapsed time per image
was computed. Input preprocessing was not included in the
timing as inputs were provided directly in tensor form. All
measurements were performed using FP32 precision.
Because no embedded devices (e.g., Jetson, Raspberry
Pi) were benchmarked, the latency results should be inter-
preted only as relative comparisons between models, not as
direct indicators of real-time performance on edge hardware.
Although GPU synchronization was not explicitly enforced
during timing, all models were evaluated under identical con-
ditions. Therefore, the reported latency values remain valid
for relative comparison across architectures.
Unless otherwise specified, all architectures were trained
under the same preprocessing, augmentation, and optimiza-
tion settings, ensuring a controlled and fair comparison across
models.
IV. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
A. PRIMARY RESULTS
The results demonstrate that modern lightweight convolu-
tional architectures can achieve high performance in surface
material classification while substantially reducing compu-
tational cost. Among the tested models, ShuffleNetV2 ×
1.0 and MobileNetV3-Small attained accuracies of 97.0%
and 96.3%, respectively, closely matching the 97.0–97.8%
range of the more complex EfficientNet variants (B0–B2)
despite using an order of magnitude fewer parameters.
Specifically, ShuffleNetV2 × 1.0 achieved an average
inference latency of 4.08 ms per image with only 1.36 M
parameters, representing an 82% reduction in parameter
count and a 45% lower latency compared to EfficientNet-
B2 (7.85 M, 7.37 ms), while maintaining accuracy within
0.8 percentage points of the best-performing model. Table 1
summarizes accuracy and efficiency metrics for all evaluated
architectures. EfficientNet-B2 achieved the highest mean
accuracy (97.81%) but at the cost of 7.85 M parameters and
0.69 B FLOPs. In contrast, ShuffleNetV2 × 1.0 reached a
comparable 97.04% accuracy while requiring over 80% fewer
parameters and nearly half the inference latency, making it
the optimal baseline for subsequent experiments. Smaller
MobileNetV3 and ShuffleNetV2 variants further reduced
FLOPs and storage with minor accuracy loss, an important
trade-off for memory or power-constrained platforms [19].

LMT-108 dataset. This substantial performance gap indicates
that, in small-sample texture classification settings, augmen-
tation is critical for mitigating overfitting and enabling the
learning of invariant surface representations. While part of
this gain can be attributed to the limited number of training
samples per class, the result highlights the strong depen-

data diversity. Accordingly, augmentation is treated as a
fixed component of the training pipeline in all subsequent

classification. Unlike object-centric recognition tasks that
benefit from deep hierarchical abstraction, texture recog-
nition primarily relies on the discrimination of localized,
repetitive patterns and subtle differences across spatial neigh-
borhoods [43]. Architectures that efficiently preserve and mix
local features therefore exhibit a structural advantage on the
LMT-108 dataset.
Classical CNNs such as AlexNet and ResNet-18 rely on
dense convolutional blocks and progressive depth to increase
representational power. While effective for general-purpose
vision tasks, this design can lead to redundant parameteriza-
tion for texture-centric problems, where increasing network
depth does not necessarily increase model discriminative
capacity [44]. Consequently, these architectures incur sub-
stantial computational overhead without proportional gains
in classification accuracy.
EfficientNet demonstrates strong representational capacity
through compound scaling of depth, width, and resolution
[18]. However, this compound scaling strategy implicitly
favors deeper feature hierarchies, which results in dimin-
ishing returns for small-scale texture datasets. The small
performance gains observed with increasing EfficientNet
depth also suggest that beyond a certain capacity, adding
additional layers mostly amplifies redundancy rather than
improving texture classification accuracy, while increasing
inference latency and memory access cost.
In contrast, ShuffleNetV2 is explicitly optimized for
efficient feature reuse and low memory access overhead.
ShuffleNetV2 employs channel splitting and shuffling to
reduce memory access cost while maintaining cross-channel
information interaction [17], resulting in strong texture clas-
sification performance despite its fewer parameters. This
design enables ShuffleNetV2 ×1.0 to achieve accuracy com-
parable to EfficientNet-B2 while requiring over 80% fewer
parameters and significantly lower inference latency, indicat-
ing that effective channel mixing can compensate for reduced
model depth in texture classification settings.
MobileNet architectures further emphasize efficiency
through depthwise-separable convolutions and inverted resid-
ual blocks, which separate spatial filtering from channel-wise
feature mixing [41], [42]. MobileNetV3 also incorporates
lightweight attention mechanisms and optimized nonlinear-
ities, resulting in low latency across all evaluated variants.

Interestingly, ResNet-18 exhibits a relatively fast inference

newer, lightweight models.
Overall, these results indicate that architectural strategies
explicitly designed for efficient feature reuse, such as channel
shuffling, inverted residuals, and depthwise-separable convo-
lutions, are particularly well suited for surface texture clas-
sification under constrained computational budgets. Among
the evaluated architectures, ShuffleNetV2 ×1.0 achieves a
favorable balance between accuracy, efficiency, and real-time
performance on LMT-108 texture scans, supporting its poten-
tial use in real-time and embedded applications.

V. CONCLUSION
This study presented a systematic evaluation of lightweight
convolutional architectures for surface material classification
and showed that compact models can achieve strong per-
formance while substantially reducing computational cost.
Although EfficientNet-B2 achieved the highest accuracy,
the analysis demonstrated that ShuffleNetV2 and MobileNet
variants provide the most favorable accuracy–efficiency
trade-off, with ShuffleNetV2 ×1.0 offering competitive per-
formance at a fraction of the parameters and latency. The
scenario-based evaluation further highlighted that no sin-
gle architecture is universally optimal, emphasizing that
backbone selection must align with specific deployment con-
straints rather than accuracy alone. These findings reduce
the effective architecture search space to a small set of con-
sistently strong candidates for each deployment scenario,
enabling practitioners to select suitable backbones without
exhaustive experimentation. The supplementary visuo-tactile
experiment also showed that the strongest lightweight visual
backbone generalizes effectively to multimodal fusion with
minimal additional computational overhead. Future work will
explore improved augmentation strategies, dynamic feature
integration mechanisms for lightweight architectures, and
hardware-level benchmarking on embedded platforms.
