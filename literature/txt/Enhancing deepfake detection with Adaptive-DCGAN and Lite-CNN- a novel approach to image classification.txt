Abstract
The rapid advancement of digital content generation technologies has led to the 
proliferation of deepfake media, posing significant security and privacy risks on 
social networks. Conventional deepfake detection methods struggle with accuracy, 
computational efficiency, and adaptability to evolving fake image generation 
techniques. To address these challenges, we propose a novel deepfake detection 
framework that integrates an Adaptive deep convolutional generative adversarial 
network (Adaptive-DCGAN) with a lightweight convolutional neural network (Lite-
CNN). The Adaptive-DCGAN generates high-quality synthetic images, enhancing 
the training dataset, while the Lite-CNN efficiently classifies real and fake images 
with minimal computational overhead. Experimental results demonstrate that the 
proposed model achieves 95% accuracy, significantly outperforming existing GAN-
CNN-based detection systems, which only reach 53% accuracy. Additionally, the 
framework exhibits superior performance metrics, including 95% precision, recall, 
and F1-score, ensuring robust and reliable deepfake detection. The proposed system 
offers a scalable and efficient solution for securing digital media platforms against 
deepfake threats. Future work will focus on improving generalization across diverse 
datasets, incorporating advanced synthetic image generation techniques, and 
enabling real-time deepfake detection for large-scale social media applications.

2  Related work
Science and technology have created Deepfake technology by using AI to make synthetic 
media look realistic, yet this innovation leads to multiple harmful social consequences 
affecting all groups in society. The main disadvantage of deepfake technology exists in 
its ability to spread false information because users can create fake news and fake evi­
dence and impersonate other individuals with deceptive intent. The integrity of media 
is under threat because of this which causes public distrust and makes fact-checking 
operations more complicated. Deepfakes create a substantial threat to privacy together 
with security which substantially endangers public figures who face the possibility of 
libel or blackmail attacks. Deepfake technology serves as a powerful political tool which 
authorities can use to spark social chaos and dissolving governments. Effective detec­
tion and mitigation systems need rapid development to prevent synthetic media from 
misuses since they create dangerous effects on society. The critical requirement exists 
for thorough detection systems which can efficiently spot and handle evolving deepfake 
techniques during real-time operation.
The paper [2] establishes a deep learning hybrid model which joins Generative Adver­
sarial Networks and ResNet50 to advance fake face detection capabilities. The GANs 
produce authentic synthetic images while adding channel-wise attention features to 
ResNet50 for detailed extraction capabilities. To maintain training stability preprocess­
ing methods including normalization and augmentation procedures are employed. The 
model reaches 83% accuracy because it performs better than VGG16 and ResNet50 
when detecting counterfeit facial images. The author team clarifies that additional 
research should focus on testing their model using various and larger datasets as well as 
defending against adversarial attacks and investigating optimization methods for obtain­
ing additional accuracy improvements. The authors are pursuing cross-database evalua­
tions which will test the model’s real-world application capabilities.

As shown in Fig. 4 our proposed Adaptive-DCGAN framework consists of three main 
components: the Generator, Discriminator, and Adaptive-DCGAN model.
The Generator takes a random noise vector and progressively transforms it into a syn­
thetic image using dense layers, batch normalization, and activation functions like ReLU 
and Tanh. The Discriminator, on the other hand, is a CNN-based binary classifier that 
distinguishes between real and fake images. It utilizes LeakyReLU activation, dropout 
layers, and an adaptive learning rate (Exponential Decay) to improve training stability. 
The Adaptive-DCGAN integrates both models into a unified system, where the discrim­
inator is frozen during generator training, allowing adversarial learning. This adaptive 
approach improves training stability, prevents mode collapse, and enhances the realism 
of generated images.
Our proposed Lite-CNN model (shown in Fig. 5) is a lightweight yet effective neural 
network designed for real vs. fake image classification.
It consists of two convolutional layers with 32 and 64 filters, followed by batch nor­
malization and 4×4 max-pooling for feature extraction. The network then flattens the 
extracted features and passes them through a fully connected dense layer with 128 units, 
incorporating L2 regularization (0.01) and a dropout rate of 0.9 to reduce overfitting. 
The final output layer uses a SoftMax activation function for binary classification. The 
model is optimized using Adam optimizer (learning rate = 0.0001) and categorical cross-
entropy loss, with an adaptive learning rate scheduler to enhance performance. This 
CNN model efficiently classifies images with high accuracy while maintaining low com­
putational cost, making it ideal for detecting GAN-generated fake images.
The Lite-CNN architecture is intentionally designed as a lightweight model to ensure 
deployment feasibility in resource-constrained environments, such as mobile forensics 
and real-time social media monitoring systems. While the architecture is shallow com­
pared to deeper networks like ResNet or EfficientNet, it is specifically optimized for the 
targeted detection task by leveraging data augmentation through Adaptive-DCGAN-
generated synthetic samples. The simplicity of Lite-CNN offers significant benefits in

3.3.1  Adaptive mechanism justification
The core novelty of our Adaptive-DCGAN lies in its dynamic training adjustment mech­
anism, which aims to maintain equilibrium between the generator and discriminator 
during adversarial learning a known challenge in GAN-based systems due to training 
instability and mode collapse.
The adaptation process is guided by a normalized deviation metric:
Deviation = AC −ACV
ACV

(2)
where AC denotes the conditional (target) performance value, and ACV represents the 
current observed performance. This formulation was chosen because it is scale-indepen­
dent, allowing the mechanism to operate consistently across datasets of varying sizes and 
quality distributions. By using a relative rather than absolute difference, the model can 
detect proportionally significant performance changes regardless of baseline accuracy.
Threshold (TH)  selection: The threshold TH defines the minimum deviation required 
to trigger an adjustment. It was determined empirically through a grid search over mul­
tiple runs, with the objective of maximizing training stability while avoiding over-sensi­
tivity to normal stochastic fluctuations. This is consistent with best practices in adaptive 
learning systems, where parameter tuning is performed in a data-driven yet stability-
conscious manner.
Adjustment factor (k) selection: Once a deviation exceeding TH is detected, an 
adaptive factor k (ranging from 0.1 to 0.9) modifies the learning rate and other train­
ing parameters. This factor was optimized through iterative experimentation to ensure 
smooth convergence and to prevent either the generator or discriminator from domi­
nating. This concept parallels proportional–integral control in signal processing, where 
gradual corrective adjustments help stabilize oscillatory systems.
Unlike conventional learning rate schedulers (e.g., step decay or cosine annealing), 
which follow a fixed schedule, our adaptive mechanism responds in real time to perfor­
mance fluctuations, enabling targeted corrections during adversarial learning.
Empirical validation: The ablation study in Sect. 4.6 confirms the practical benefit of 
this mechanism: incorporating the adaptive learning module improved accuracy and 
F1-score by +4% compared to the static-learning-rate Adaptive-DCGAN. Additionally, 
convergence curves were smoother, and discriminator overpowering was reduced, indi­
cating a more balanced adversarial process.

4  Empirical results and discussion
4.1  Datasets
We utilize the “Real and Fake Face Detection” dataset from Kaggle as the primary foun­
dation for our deepfake detection operations. This dataset comprises 2041 JPG images, 
including 960 fake images generated using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and 
1081 real photographs. It is publicly accessible at Kaggle [26] and is widely recognized 
in deepfake detection research. Due to its balanced distribution of real and synthetic 
images, the dataset provides a robust foundation for training Convolutional Neural Net­
works (CNNs) and evaluating GAN-based detection models. The diverse collection of 
images enhances the generalizability of the proposed model, leading to improved accu­
racy in distinguishing authentic images from digitally manipulated ones.

4.2  Performance metrics
The performance of the Adaptive-DCGAN with Lite-CNN model is evaluated using 
multiple metrics, including three distinct loss functions for the generator and discrimi­
nator, along with accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. These metrics provide a com­
prehensive assessment of the model’s ability to generate realistic images, differentiate 
between real and fake content, and maintain stable performance. 
1.	 Generator loss (G-loss): This metric evaluates how effectively the generator produces 
realistic images. Lower G-loss values indicate that the generated images are of higher 
quality and less likely to be misclassified as fake by the discriminator.
2.	 Discriminator loss (D-loss): D-loss measures the discriminator’s capability to 
distinguish real images from generated ones. Maintaining a balanced D-loss is crucial 
to ensuring stable training and preventing either the generator or discriminator from 
overpowering the other.
3.	 Accuracy: Accuracy quantifies the model’s ability to correctly classify real and 
fake images. Higher accuracy signifies improved reliability, as fewer images are 
misclassified.
4.	 Precision: Precision assesses how well the model identifies fake images by measuring 
the proportion of correctly classified fake images among all predicted fake instances. 
A higher precision value indicates that the model effectively avoids mislabeling real 
images as fake.
5.	 Recall: Recall determines the model’s ability to detect all actual fake images within 
the dataset. A high recall score signifies robust deepfake identification, reducing the 
chances of fake images going undetected.
6.	 F1-score: The F1-score balances precision and recall by providing a single performance 
metric that accounts for both false positives and false negatives. A higher F1-score 
indicates a well-optimized model with strong discriminatory capabilities.
4.3  Results
We implemented and tested the proposed Adaptive-DCGAN alongside Lite-CNN for 
deepfake detection, and performance evaluations demonstrate its superiority over tra­
ditional detection systems. The model was trained and tested using the Kaggle“Real 
and Fake Face Detection”dataset, which comprises 2041 images - 1081 real and 960 fab­
ricated (shown in Fig. 6). Training was conducted using 80% of the dataset, with 20% 
reserved for testing.
During training, the Generator (G) maintained an average loss of 0.10, while the 
Discriminator (D) stabilized at an average loss of 1.01, indicating effective adversarial 
learning (shown in Fig. 7). When evaluated using the Lite-CNN classification model, 
the system achieved an accuracy of 95%, significantly outperforming existing detection 
methods, which only reached 53% accuracy (depicted in Fig. 8 ). Performance metrics 
further validate the robustness of our approach, achieving a Precision of 95%, Recall of 
95%, and an F1-score of 95% (depicted in Fig. 9). The reported accuracy, precision, recall, 
and F1-score are the mean values obtained across the k-fold cross-validation runs, pro­
viding a more statistically sound evaluation.

4.7  Cross-dataset generalization
While our proposed system achieves 95% accuracy on the Kaggle dataset, we acknowl­
edge the importance of evaluating generalization across multiple datasets. Cross-dataset 
validation is critical in deepfake detection research due to the diversity of manipulation 
techniques, post-processing artifacts, and compression schemes present in real-world 
content.
Existing studies show that even high-performing models like XceptionNet suffer per­
formance drops when evaluated on unseen datasets. For example, XceptionNet trained 
on FaceForensics++ achieves 99.26% accuracy but drops to 48.2% on Celeb-DF v2 with­
out fine-tuning [29]. This illustrates the challenges of generalization and the need for 
adaptive or ensemble-based approaches.
In future work, we aim to evaluate our model on challenging datasets such as Celeb-
DF v2, FaceForensics++, and DFDC, to better assess robustness to unseen manipula­
tions. Additionally, expanding the dataset diversity during training and incorporating 
self-supervised learning strategies may enhance generalizability without significantly 
increasing computational cost.
5  Conclusion and future work
The proposed Adaptive-DCGAN model demonstrates significantly superior per­
formance over existing GAN-CNN systems in distinguishing real from fake images. 
Achieving a Generator (G) loss of 0.10 and a Discriminator (D) loss of 1.01 highlights 
the model’s stable training process, leading to the generation of highly realistic synthetic 
images. A remarkable improvement in detection accuracy was observed, with the pro­
posed model achieving 95% accuracy compared to the 53% accuracy of the previous 
system. Furthermore, the Adaptive-DCGAN exhibits outstanding classification metrics, 
including precision (95%), recall (95%), and F1-score (95%), confirming its reliability in 
accurately identifying real and fake images. These results establish Adaptive-DCGAN as 
a robust and effective solution for real vs. fake image classification, reducing misclas­
sification errors and enhancing deepfake detection capabilities. Unlike prior GAN-CNN 
based detection frameworks, our Adaptive-DCGAN with Lite-CNN offers a scalable, 
resource-efficient, and dynamically adaptive solution capable of responding to evolving 
deepfake techniques, ensuring practical deployment in real-world scenarios where com­
putational constraints and content diversity present significant challenges.
Future research should focus on optimizing the model for diverse datasets to improve 
its adaptability across various real-world applications. Enhancing the model’s robust­
ness by incorporating advanced deepfake imagery generated by architectures such as 
StyleGAN and ProGAN will further strengthen its detection capabilities. Integrating 
self-supervised learning techniques can reduce dependence on labeled datasets, mak­
ing the system more flexible and scalable. Additionally, to facilitate large-scale deepfake 
detection in social media security and digital forensics, real-time implementation with 
computational optimizations should be explored to enhance efficiency and reduce pro­
cessing costs.
