With the help of some modern image generative techniques, it is possible to generate or manipulate 
image or video contents without introducing any obvious visual artifacts. If these manipulated 
images/videos are abused, it probably has a huge negative impact on society and individuals. Thus, 
deepfake detection has attracted considerable attention in recent years. Although the existing methods 
can achieve good detection performance on high-quality datasets, they are still far from satisfactory 
for low-quality dataset and cross-dataset evaluation. In this paper, therefore, we propose a new CNN-
based method via multi-scale convolution and vision transformer for deepfake detection. In the proposed 
model, we design a multi-scale module with dilation convolution and depthwise separable convolution to 
capture more face details and tampering artifacts at different scales. Unlike the traditional classiﬁcation 
module, furthermore, we employ a vision transformer to further learn the global information of face 
features for classiﬁcation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that in most cases the proposed method 
achieves better detection results on both high-quality and low-quality datasets compared with related 
modern methods, and the cross-dataset generalization capabilities of the proposed method are good. In 
addition, many ablation experiments are provided to verify the rationality of the proposed network.

1. Introduction
With the rapid development of some modern image generative 
techniques, especially for generative adversarial networks (GANs), 
more and more sophisticated deepfake methods have been pro-
vided to generate or manipulate realistic faces that people can 
not distinguish them from real ones. Nowadays, many open source 
code1,2 and mobile applications (e.g., FaceApp3 and Zao4) are 
available so that we can easily and effectively create manipulated 
face images and videos without any specialized knowledge [41]. If 
these face faces are used for nefarious purposes, for things like re-
venge, porn or fraud, which would have a huge negative impact on 
society and individuals. Thus, it is important to develop effective 
methods for deepfake detection.

real applications so that the quality would decline. As illustrated in

drop signiﬁcantly. Up to now, the detection performances of ex-

images. In addition, their generalization performances need further 
enhancement too.

scale convolution and vision transformer for deepfake detection. 
Speciﬁcally, we employ a dual-subnet structure to catch high-
level face semantic information and employ a vision transformer 
module for the ﬁnal classiﬁcation in the proposed model. The 
dual-subnet includes a multi-scale module branch and a MBConv

2. Relative work
In this section, we will give a brief overview of deepfake tech-
nique, deepfake datasets, and deepfake detection methods that 
used in recent literatures.
2.1. DeepFake technique
Typically, the existing deepfake techniques can be divided into 
4 categories [41]: entire face synthesis, identity swap, attribute 
manipulation, and expression swap. Entire face synthesis aims to 
generate entire non-existent face images, the typical methods in-
clude the StyleGAN [16] and PGGAN [15]; identity swap tries to 
swap the face of one person (source face) in videos with the face 
of another person (target face), such as FSGAN [32] and FaceShifter
[21]; attribute manipulation aims to modify some attributes (e.g., 
hair or skin) of the target face, such as StarGAN [5] and AttGAN 
[12]; expression swap tries to change the facial expression (e.g., 
excitement and frustration) of the target face, such as NeuralTex-
tures [39] and Face2Face [40].
Among the four categories, identity swap is currently recog-
nized as the most harmful and widely studied type of face tam-
pering [41]. For tampering a video, this technique usually includes 
three steps: it ﬁrstly divides the video into frames and locates 
the faces in each frame; and then it swaps the face of a per-
son (i.e., source face) in a frame with the face of another person 
(i.e., target face) through some deep learning methods. Finally, it 
applies some image fusion techniques to stitch face images to re-
duce tampering artifact and synthesize a fake video. Up to now, 
many effective identity swap methods have been proposed. For in-
stance, Korshunova et al. [19] proposed the ﬁrst face swap method 
to transform the identity. They use convolution neural networks to 
capture the appearance of the target images and propose a new 
loss function to produce highly realistic results; Olszewski et al. 
[33] propose a method to realistically puppeteer and animate a 
face from RGB images using a source video sequence; Li et al. 
[21] try to generate the swapped face with high-quality by ex-
ploiting and integrating the target attributes thoroughly and adap-
tively, and propose a novel two-stage face swapping method, called 
FaceShiter.
2.2. DeepFake datasets
Up to now, there are some public deepfake datasets avail-
able online. As illustrated in Table 1, we will describe six typical 
datasets that are commonly used for comparative studies in recent 
literatures in the following.
• UADFV [23] includes 49 real videos from Youtube, which are 
then used to generate 49 fake videos. These videos are about 
11.14 seconds, and each video represents one identity and has 
the resolution of 294 × 500.

videos that are downloaded from the Internet, meaning that

probably have been compressed more than one time. Thus, the

Note that the numbers of videos in UADFV and Deepfake-TIMIT 
are relatively fewer, and the modern deepfake detection methods 
can achieve better performances on the two datasets. In this paper, 
therefore, we employ the other 4 datasets (i.e., Celeb-DF, DFDC, 
FaceFornesics++, and WildDeepfake) that are more diﬃcult to de-
tect in our experiments.
2.3. Deepfake detection method
In the past several years, many CNN-based methods, such as 
[1,20,29,31,22,3,26,27,14,37,24,34], have been proposed for deek-
fake detection. For example, Afchar et al. [1] focused on the meso-
scopic properties of images, and proposed a MesoNet to detect 
online videos; Hguyen et al. [31] ﬁrst introduced the capsule net-
works to detect forged images and videos; Li et al. [20] analyzed 
the differences among the color components within the gener-
ated images for classiﬁcation; Rössler et al. [35] ﬁrst introduced 
the Xception model into deepfake detection, and show that the 
detection performances of Xception can outperform most related 
methods at that time; Li et al. [24] proposed a new method that 
can effectively distinguish manipulated videos from real videos 
without using fake images as negative training; Qian et al. [34] em-
ployed two different but complementary frequency-aware clues to 
describe subtle forgery artifacts and proposed a new method (F3-
Net) for deepfake detection; Zhao et al. [45] formulated deepfake 
detection as a ﬁne-grained classiﬁcation problem and proposed a 
new multi-attentional deepfake detection network; Li et al. [22]
proposed a new image representation called Face-X-Ray to show 
the blending boundary for a forged image and the absence of 
blending for a real image, and achieved a high detection accuracy 
and generalization performance in high-quality videos; Castillo et 
al. [3] proposed two different methods of initializing convolutional 
neural networks for image manipulation detection; Shang et al.

[37] proposed a Pixel-Region Relation Network (PRRNet) to cap-
ture pixel-wise and region-wise relations respectively for deepfake 
detection; Jia et al. [14] proposed an inconsistency-aware wavelet 
dual-branch network to detect inter-image and intra-image incon-
sistencies in deepfake; Liu et al. [26] employed spatial image and 
phase spectrum to capture the up-sampling artifacts of deepfake 
to improve the transferability, and achieved the very good results 
on multi-class classiﬁcation on FaceFornesics++ dataset.
Note that some modern methods, such as Dual-Branch [14] and 
PRRnet [37], can achieve very good performances for those high-
quality images (e.g., on Celeb-DF [25] and high-quality versions 
of FaceForensics++ [35]). However, the detection performances are 
still from satisfactory for low-quality images. In addition, the gen-
eralization ability of existing methods needs further improvement.
3. Proposed method
As illustrated in Fig. 2, The network architecture includes 
four different modules, that is, pre-processing module, multi-scale 
module, MBConv block module, and vision transformer module. 
Note that here we select the EﬃcientNet-b4 [38] as the back-
bone of the proposed method (see the pre-processing module 
and MBConv block module) since the EﬃcientNet-b4 can provide 
a good trade-off in dimensionality, computing time, and classi-
ﬁcation performance on deepfake detection. Thus EﬃcientNet-b4 
usually serves as a backbone or baseline in many existing litera-
tures. In the following, we will describe the four modules in detail.
3.1. Pre-processing module
The pre-processing module includes a 3 × 3 convolution, 2 
MBConv1 Blocks and 8 MBConv6 Blocks, which is deﬁned in 
EﬃcientNet-B4 [38]. As reported in [38], the EﬃcitentNet-b4 is 
one version of the EﬃcientNet, which consists of 2 classical con-
volutions, 2 MBConv1 blocks, 30 MBConv6 blocks and a fully con-
nected layer. Besides, the EﬃcientNet-b4 has good image classiﬁca-
tion performance with 19 millions of parameters and 4.2 billions 
of FLOPs and reaches the 83.8% top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet 
database. The network architecture of MBConv1 block and MB-
Conv6 block which are deﬁned in EﬃcientNet-b4 are as illustrated 
in Fig. 3. Note that the MBConv1 block consists of Depthwise con-
volution, Batch Normalization (BN), Squeeze-and-Excitation mod-
ule (SE) [13] and Pointwise convolution & BN. Compared with the 
MBConv1 block, the MBConv6 block adds a Pointwise convolution 
and a BN layer.
For a face image, the pre-processing module aims to produce a 
preliminary feature map f p ∈RH×W ×C , where (H, W ) is the fea-
ture resolution and C is the number of the feature channel. Then 
f p is then fed into a dual-subnet in the proposed model.
3.2. Dual-subnet: multi-scale module & MBConv block module
In the proposed network architecture, we employ a dual-subnet 
structure, that is, a multi-scale module and a MBConv block mod-
ule, to deal with the feature map f p which is obtained in the pre-
processing module. The multi-scale module aims to learn multi-
scale face information, while the MBConv block module aims to 
learn high-dimensional face semantic information.
3.2.1. Multi-scale module
Most existing CNN-based methods for deepfake detection, such 
as [1,29,31], usually stack single-scale convolutional layers to ex-
tract high-dimensional face semantic information. In the proposed 
method, however, we propose a multi-scale module with differ-
ent dilation convolutions and depthwise separable convolutions in

3.2.2. MBConv block module
The proposed MBConv block module follows the same setting 
that are used in the EﬃcientNet-B4 [38]. Speciﬁcally, we stack 22 
MBConv6 Blocks together to obtain a high-dimensional face se-
mantic feature map f2 for face forgery detection. Note that the 
MBConv Block architecture [36] (both MBConv1 block and MB-

3.3. Vision transformer module
Most existing methods, including the EﬃcientNet-b4 and meth-
ods [1,31,27,14], usually feed the concatenated feature map (i.e., 
f1 ⊗f2) into a global average pooling and full connection for the 
ﬁnal classiﬁcation. Inspired by [7], we employ a vision transformer 
module for classiﬁcation. Firstly, we use a separableConv block to 
increase the dimension of f1 ⊗f2 and output fin. Since the stan-
dard transformer receives a 1D sequence of token embedding as 
input, we reshape fin into f ′
in as it did in [10]. Then, we ﬂatten 
f ′
in and map the ﬂattened f ′
in into D dimensions by trainable liner 
projection, where the value of D is the size of the constant hid-
den vector which is used in the all layers of transformer. And then 
the position embeddings are added to the patch embedding to re-
tain the positional information. The result of embedding is used 
as the input of the transformer encoder. The transformer encoder 
is composed of alternating layers of multi-head self-attention and 
multilayer perceptron (MLP) blocks. In this way, the transformer 
can not only integrate the information of the entire feature maps 
via self-attention, but also understand the image structure through 
the learnable position embedding. Finally, we can get the ﬁnal clas-
siﬁcation result through an MLP.
4. Experiments
Four popular datasets, including Celeb-DF [25], DFDC [8], Face-
Forensics++ (denoted as FF++) [35], and WildDeepfake [48] are 
considered in our experiments. First of all, we use the MTCNN [44]
to detect and extract faces for each frame and enlarge the face re-
gion by a factor of 1.3 around the center of the detected face, and 
then we resize the face images into 320 × 320 as the input of the 
proposed method. For a fair comparison, all experimental results 
are evaluated on the same test dataset for all compared methods. 
Like most related methods, we directly use the reported perfor-
mances of existing methods for comparative study.
In training stage, we employ the cross-entropy and Adam opti-
mizer with the batch size 48. We train the proposed networks in 
total of 20 epochs and reduce the learning rate to 1/10 of the orig-
inal every 10 epochs. We set the initial learning rate to 0.0001 and 
select the model with the best accuracy as the ﬁnal model.6

4.1.4. Experiments on WildDeepfake
In this section, we use the WildDeepfake dataset for evaluation. 
Note that the WildDeepfake is a challenging real-world dataset for 
deepfake detection. Unlike other datasets, both real and fake videos

4.2. Cross-dataset evaluation
In this section, we evaluate the generalization performance of 
the proposed model. In the following, two different cross-dataset 
evaluations are included in our experiments.
4.2.1. Evaluation on unknown deepfake techniques within FF++
As described in section 2.2, there are 1,000 original real videos 
in FF++. For each original video, there are four different fake 
videos generated by four manipulated techniques, including Deep-
Fake (denoted as DF), Face2Face (i.e., F2F), FaceSwap (i.e., FS), and 
NeuralTextures (i.e., NT). In this section, we ﬁrstly train a classiﬁer 
on the images generated by one manipulated technique, and then 
use the resulting classiﬁer on other images generated by other 
three manipulated techniques. For a fair comparison, all the exper-
imental results are evaluated on raw quality images without any 
post-processing as it did in [4]. The comparative results are shown 
in Table 6. From Table 6, we have three following observations:
• If the training data and test data are generated by the same 
manipulated technique, the performances of the proposed 
method are all over 99.25%, as shown in the diagonal results 
of the table. Similar good results can be obtained for the other 
two detection methods (i.e., Xception and Res-Layer1), which 
means that the tampering artifacts introduced by the four ma-
nipulated techniques are relatively easy to detect.
• If the training dataset does not match the test dataset, the cor-
responding performances would decline in different ranges for 
the three detection methods. Taking DF as training dataset for 
instance, our detection accuracies would drop from 99.97% to 
90.32%, 44.73% and 94.53% when evaluating the F2F, FS and 
NT separately, which means that the inherent statistical prop-
erties of the fake images generated by different manipulated 
techniques are different.
• Overall, the proposed method outperforms two other detection 
methods (i.e., Xception and Res-Layer1) in most cases. The de-
tection improvements are signiﬁcant in some cases. Taking FS 
as training dataset for instance, our detection accuracy on NT 
is as high as 96.24%, while they are less than 70% for the other 
two methods, which means that the cross-dataset generaliza-
tion capabilities of the proposed method are better.
Note that when we use F2F as our training dataset, the results 
are relatively poor for FS. The main reason is that statistical differ-
ences between F2F and FS are relatively huge.
4.2.2. Evaluation on unknown dataset
As it did in most related methods (such as [34,45]), in this 
section, we ﬁrstly train the proposed model on the FF++ (HQ) 
dataset, and then use the resulting classiﬁer to evaluate those im-
ages from the Celeb-DF dataset. The comparative results are shown 
in Table 7. From Table 7, we observe that the detection results 
are lower than 68% for all existing methods, while the proposed 
method achieves result as high as 71.41%. Compared with the cur-
rent best method (i.e., multi-attention [45]), we can still achieve 
3.97% improvements, which means that the proposed method has 
much better generalization ability compared with existing ones.

4.3.1. Analysis on the proposed modules
In this section, we show the detection accuracies when an im-
portant module in the proposed model is removed or replaced, 
including vision transformer module, MBConv blocks module and 
Multi-scale block module separately. The comparative results are 
shown in Table 8. From Table 8, we can obtain three following ob-
servations:
• First of all, the proposed method can achieve the detection ac-
curacy as high as 82.63%, which are higher than three variants 
of the proposed method as well as some modern methods (re-
fer to Table 5);
• For the ﬁnal classiﬁcation, we replace the vision transformer 
module as the typical classiﬁcation module, that is the global 
average pooling and full connection. The corresponding detec-
tion accuracy will drop from 82.63% to 80.17%, which means 
that capturing the global information via vision transformer 
are helpful to detect deepfake images;
• The proposed network employs a dual-subnet architecture. 
If any one of streams in the proposed network is removed, 
i.e., Stream #1: Multi-scale Block module or Stream #2: the 
MBConv Block module, the detection accuracies will drop to 
79.53% or 78.13% respectively, which means that combining
the multi-scale face information (i.e., f1) and high-dimensional 
face semantic information (i.e., f2) is useful to enhance the de-
tection performance.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a new CNN-based method combin-
ing with multi-scale feature and vision transformer for deepfake 
detection, and show that the proposed method can achieve the 
advanced results evaluated on four popular datasets. The proposed 
method achieves the state-of-the-art result both on FF++ and Wild-
Deepfake. Taking Wilddeepfake for instance, the proposed method 
can obtain 2.76% improvement on ACC compared with the current 
best method [47]. In addition, we also show that the generalization 
ability of the proposed model is good by cross-database evaluation. 
Furthermore, we provide many ablation studies to verify the ratio-
nality of the proposed network.
In the proposed method, face images in the spatial domain are 
considered. In future, the multi-scale information will be extracted 
in both the spatial and frequency (such as DCT and/or wavelet) 
domains for further improving detection performances. Combined 
with image segmentation technology, furthermore, some content 
adaptive schemes will be integrated in the proposed network so 
that the features can be focused on different facial parts, such as 
eyes, nose, and mouth, which probably have more tampering arti-
facts.
