Abstract 
The increasing convergence of Internet of Things (IoT) devices and edge computing 
platforms in current digital networks has presented extraordinary opportunities. However, this 
surge has presented a massive attack surface susceptible to developing attacks like deepfakes. 
Deepfakes refer to realistic, but fake images, videos, and sounds made by artificial 
intelligence (AI) models. Recent developments in deepfake generation have made deepfakes 
more accurate and simpler to make. Deepfakes have previously been employed for numerous 
malicious reasons, including the publication of fabricated outcomes in scientific magazines or 
an attack on the identity tests employed by banks over artificial videos and voices, raising 
concerns regarding them and their usage. In response to this occurrence, the research group 
has selected the growth of techniques to distinguish real content from deepfakes. Machine 
learning (DL) and deep learning (DL) driven systems have attained extremely precise 
outcomes in the detection of deepfakes. Furthermore, numerous previous studies on 
perceiving deepfake images or videos were concentrated on DL. This paper presents a 
Feature Fusion Integrated with Adaptive Hidden Semi-Markov Model for Robust Deepfake 
Detection (FFAHSMM-RDD) method. The aim is to present a reliable pathway for securing 
multimedia communication and preventing deepfake-driven threats in real-world 
applications. Initially, data augmentation, image resizing, and normalization techniques are 
applied to improve image quality and enhance generalization. For feature representation, a 
fusion of three models, such as ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B4, and MobileNetV3, is used. 
Moreover, the adaptive hidden semi-Markov model (AHSMM) is employed to model 
temporal dependencies and sequence dynamics within the data effectively. In the deepfake 
classification process, a deep belief network (DBN) model is implemented to classify real and 
fake content efficiently. The performance validation of the FFAHSMM-RDD methodology 
demonstrated a superior value of 98.61% under the Celeb DF (v2) dataset 
Keywords 
Deepfake; Edge Computing; Deep Learning; Internet of Things; Deep Belief Network

3.2.1. ResNet‐50 Architecture 
The ResNet‐50 method is a deep convolutional neural network (DCNN), which consists of 50 
layers, intended to tackle the vanishing gradient issue in deep networks [21]. Its structure is 
constructed on the notion of residual learning, where shortcut connections go around one or 
many layers. These connections allow the system to learn residual mappings instead of an 
original feature mapping, permitting deeper layers to concentrate on more intricate features 
without losing gradient data. The ResNet‐50 method is a CNN, which was initially pre-
trained on the ImageNet database. The structure of the ResNet‐50 model holds 50 layers and 
is generally divided into 5 sections. The first part contains an input pre-processing layer, 
which consists of a convolutional layer. The remaining 4 parts contain Bottleneck Building 
Blocks. Every Bottleneck Building Block comprises numerous normalization layers, ReLU 
activation functions, convolutional layers, and a shortcut connection. The ResNet‐50 
technique has 49 convolutional layers and one fully connected (FC) layer in total. The design 
of Bottleneck Building Blocks, which creates ResNet‐50, is stated by 𝑦= 𝐹(𝑥) + 𝑥. In the 
given calculation, 𝐹(𝑥) is denoted as the residual function, whereas 𝑥 and 𝑦 signify the 
residual function’s input and output parameters. The structure of ResNet uses two kinds of 
building blocks, regular and modified. The regular building blocks are employed in ResNet‐
34 and hold dual layers of 3𝑋3 convolutional layers. 
The bottleneck building blocks are a specific alteration employed in deeper ResNet structures 
like ResNet‐50, ResNet‐101, and ResNet‐152. These blocks contain a series of 3 layers, such 
as 1 × 1,3 × 3, and 1 × 1 convolutional layers. The dual 1 × 1 layers aid in decreasing and 
then enlarging the sizes, thus narrowing the flow’s dimensions. This makes a bottleneck 
effect, which provides a Bottleneck Building Block. This modification permits a deeper 
network to be trained without considerably enlarging the time of training. The feature 
mapping of ResNet‐50 exposes intricate and expanded activation patterns in the deeper layer 
owing to its deep structure and residual connections.

3.4. DBN-based Classification 
In the deepfake classification process, a DBN is employed to classify real and fake content 
[25] effectively. This method is chosen for its effectiveness in learning hierarchical, 
generative data transformation from the fused CNN and AHSMM models, thus capturing 
intrinsic associations in deepfake patterns. This model is also considered for its efficiency 
over conventional classifiers. Furthermore, this model is efficient for computation, and unlike 
transformers, they are excellent in modelling high-level abstractions. This mitigates 
overfitting and stabilizes learning. The DBN model also enhances accuracy and robustness 
against subtle changes in videos, while also extracting probabilistic latent features. 
DBN is formed by many layers of stacked Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM), proficient 
in removing deeper features from the original data. Pre-training comprises unsupervised 
learning; here, the procedure initiates the input data passed into the primary RBM. The output 
from the primary RBM turns the input for the second RBM, and this layer-wise training 
remains until each layer is completely trained. After pre‐training, the parameters of every 
layer, like biases and weights, were employed by the primary values for the parameters of the 
network. The training model initiates with a low‐level RBM, which acquires the inputs of 
DBN and develops higher levels by the hierarchy till the final RBM is trained at the topmost 
layers that comprises the output of DBN. In this RBM, the middle and visible layer 𝐼ℎ and 𝐼𝑣 
comprise random dual parameters in the middle and upper layers, respectively. Furthermore,

5. Conclusion 
In this article, the FFAHSMM-RDD model is presented. The FFAHSMM-RDD approach is 
designed to provide a reliable pathway for safeguarding multimedia communication and 
preventing deepfake-based threats in real-world applications. Primarily, the FFAHSMM-
RDD approach uses data augmentation, image resizing, and normalization methods to 
enhance image quality and improve generality. For the feature extraction, a fusion of three 
methods, including ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B4, and MobileNetV3, is utilized for capturing 
diverse and discriminative features. Furthermore, the AHSMM approach is leveraged for

effectively modeling temporal dependencies and sequence dynamics in the data. In the 
deepfake classification process, a DBN is employed to effectively classify real and fake 
content. The performance validation of the FFAHSMM-RDD methodology demonstrated a 
superior value of 98.61% under the Celeb DF (v2) dataset. Limitations exist in low-quality 
videos and computationally heavy videos due to various CNN and DBN layers. Future work 
must explore lightweight, attention-based temporal, and self-supervised learning models for 
improving generalization and efficiency.
